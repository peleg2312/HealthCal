# HealthCal project guide

HealthCal is a calorie, nutrition, training and hydration tracker for iOS, Android and web from one Expo codebase. It is local-first: the device holds the working copy, an optional Supabase backend adds accounts, sync and AI food analysis.

## Product principles (from user research on existing apps)

1. Logging speed wins. The fastest path (recents, favourites, one-tap add, copy a previous meal) must never be more than two taps away.
2. Honest numbers. Calorie burn and AI food estimates are estimates: show them as a value with a range or a plain label, never false precision. Exercise adds net calories (above resting), never gross, so nothing is counted twice.
3. Trust the data source. Every food shows where its numbers come from (bundled reference, community, your own, AI estimate). Community and AI data rank below reference data.
4. No shame. Neutral, factual copy. Going over a target is information, not failure: use the warning (amber) colour, never red alarm styling, never "You failed" or "cheat". Streaks forgive a missed day.
5. Reminders are useful or silent. They carry live numbers ("620 ml left"), respect quiet hours, and skip themselves when the goal is already met.
6. Everything reversible. Deletes are soft and offer Undo via `toast()`. Irreversible actions (delete account, clear data) confirm with `ConfirmSheet`.
7. Free where it runs on your phone. Everything the app does on the device (logging, search, barcode, the diary, water, training, health sync, progress, reminders, every look and full export), and the account with backup, sync and friends, is free for good, with no ads. Only what costs money each time it runs, the AI estimate and the AI coach, has a paid plan, Plus, and the free plan keeps a daily allowance of both. The offer is shown only where someone looks for it or reaches a limit, never over logging; it states the price, the period and that it renews before anyone pays; cancelling is one step; and nothing that runs on the phone becomes paid.

## UI kit (`import { ... } from '@/ui'`)

The visual identity is "Pour": the day fills up like a cup. Every amount is a flat colour poured into an open, straight-sided vessel, topped by a crisp level line (the `rim` colour), with what is left visible above it. The cup is a tumbler: straight sides, a round base, an open top with no lip. The rules for each piece are in its source comments (`src/ui/Vessel.tsx` for the cups, `src/theme/tokens.ts` for colours, type and the cup corner).

- Layout: `Screen` (props: `header`, `footer`, `tabs`, `scroll`, `padded`; `backdrop` is accepted but ignored, there is no glow), `Header` (`title`, `subtitle`, `back`, `close`, `right`; the header of every pushed screen), `SectionTitle`, `goBack()`, `TAB_BAR_SPACE`. The four tabs use `TabHeader` from `@/components/TabHeader` instead (see "The top of a screen").
- Surfaces: `Card` (`onPress`, `tone` surface/alt/transparent, `padding`; cup corners, 1 px border, flat; `gradient` is ignored), `Sheet` (a lid: 6 px top corners, the only shadow), `ConfirmSheet`, `Divider`.
- Sheets drag down: dragging the handle or the title of any `Sheet` or `ConfirmSheet` (the `sheet-drag` zone) moves it with the finger, and letting go past a quarter of its height or flicking down calls the same `onClose` as the backdrop (Escape on web too); otherwise it settles back. It is a PanResponder on that zone only, so it works inside the native Modal and with touch or a mouse on web, and the sheet's body still scrolls. Under reduced motion the sheet does not follow the finger but still closes. A sheet that must not close passes an `onClose` that does nothing and the drag settles back. VoiceOver's escape gesture closes it too. Keep controls out of the title (the drag zone takes the touch at once) and do not wrap a sheet's content in another responder that claims every touch.
- Text: `Text` with `variant` (display, h1, h2, h3, title, body, bodyStrong, label, caption, overline, numXL, numL, numM, unit), `color` (any theme colour name), `tabular`. Headings and every num variant are Bricolage Grotesque, the rest Hanken Grotesk. A unit is a nested `<Text variant="unit">` inside the number. `Text` keeps a number with its unit, a macro letter with its number ("P 102 g"), a portion ("1.5 × 1/2 cup") and a range ("59-79 kg") on one line (`keepTogether` from `@/lib/text`, no-break spaces and word joiners), so a line of numbers breaks only at its words and separators: build lines with plain spaces and let `Text` do it; a string drawn by anything else (an SVG label, a placeholder) needs `keepTogether` itself. Browser tests that read text normalise `\u00A0` and drop `\u2060`, and the direction marks `\u200E`, `\u2068` and `\u2069`. `Text` and the text fields cap the phone's text-size setting at `MAX_FONT_SCALE` (1.3) so large text grows without breaking a row; web ignores it, so check it on a phone.
- Actions: `Button` (variant primary/secondary/ghost/danger/soft, size sm/md/lg, `icon`, `tint` for a data-coloured edge, `loading`), `IconButton` (44 square cup by default), `PressableScale` (`dimDisabled`; `hitSlop` widens the touch target on web as well as natively, without changing the look or the layout), `Chip` (`dropdown`), `ChipRow`, `Segmented` (underline tabs), `Badge`.
- Input: `TextField`, `NumberField` (`value: number | null`), `Stepper` (`compact` for a row that also holds a label on a 320 px phone), `parseNumber`. A field's real input covers its whole cup (its side room is the input's own padding; `left` and `suffix` are drawn over it without taking touches), and on web the label and cup are one `<label>`, so every tap is the platform's own tap on the field and the keyboard opens the native way on iPhone and Android browsers. Never wrap a field in something that catches the touch and calls `focus()` from code (an iPhone may not raise its keyboard for that); onboarding's `BigNumberField` and `BigTextField` follow the same rule (the input over the whole cup, centred on the unseen sizer by the unit's measured width). `Screen` keeps the focused field and the pinned footer above the keyboard: natively with KeyboardAvoidingView ("padding" on both platforms, the reasons in its comment), in phone browsers from `window.visualViewport` (`keyboardCover`, `revealDelta` in `src/ui/keyboardInset.ts`). `scripts/dev/e2e.cjs` taps every onboarding field and food search at the cup's left, middle and right and fakes the keyboard. A button beside a field matches the field's 50 px height (`style={{ height: 50 }}`). A one-line field's placeholder must fit the field at 320 px (the browser clips it mid-word): give narrow phones a shorter one, as food search does. A number field never shows a plausible value as its placeholder ("165" in an empty weight field reads as an answer already given, cannot be deleted, and Continue then shows errors under fields that look filled): an empty number field shows its unit only, or a placeholder no one could take for an answer ("DD", "e.g. 350").
- Lists: `ListRow`, `ToggleRow` and `Toggle` (the Pour switch: a 40 x 24 cup with a square-ish knob, the same on every platform, never the platform's pill), `Avatar` (cup tile: `initials`, `emoji` or an icon), `EmptyState` (draws an empty dashed cup; `emoji` is ignored), `Stat` (`color` gives a cup key, the number stays text-coloured), `CupKey` (legend key and data-source mark: filled, outline, dashed). A list that shows only its first rows opens the rest with a small ghost `Button` (chevron down or up) centred under the list, never an underlined text link, and a section's way to another screen is a ghost sm `Button` with an icon or a chevron (Train's "All activities", Water's "Edit sizes") or a row with a chevron at its end (Train's "Bring in workouts", `train-bring-in`, `@/features/activity/BringIn`, words in `bringIn.ts`: one card row under Log activity and Workout that opens a sheet with automatic sync `train-health-sync`, Import workouts `train-import-garmin` and, where sync cannot read steps, Add steps from your Health export `train-steps-import`). Deleting a record from a list row (a diary entry, a water row, a workout, a saved meal, a saved routine) and a set from a strength workout is the trash can: `Trash2` at 18 px in `textSecondary`, stroke 1.9, in a clear `IconButton` 44 px to touch.
- Amounts: `Vessel` (the open cup: `value`, `max`, `headroom` hatched at the top, amber lip when over, `labels` at the height they describe, `children` under the labels; `lip` is accepted but ignored), `MiniCup` (week strips and lists, 30 x 38), `Trough` (the sideways vessel), `ProgressBar` (flat fill with the rim tick; over target turns amber), `Hatch`. All four take `goalKind` ('limit' by default, 'reach' for a goal that is meant to be reached) and `onReached`; see "Reaching a goal" under Design rules.
- Data viz: `BarChart` (slim cups with a rim, amber above `goal`, and a bar over its line wears its level line on its top heavier and wider than the bar (also a stacked bar whose top segment is the amber, as Progress draws calories), so an over day is told without its colour; `openIndex` hatched; `highlightIndex` prints that bar's value and never dims the others, so every finished bar keeps its colour and its rim), `LineChart`, `Sparkline`, `MacroBar`. `goalKind="reach"` on `BarChart` for a goal that is not a limit (water, minutes): nothing turns amber, and a bar at or past the goal wears the brim line (its rim 1 to 2 px wider each side and 1 px heavier). Bar fills are drawn at `useTheme().chartFill` (42% on the light papers, 70% in dark, where 42% would turn every colour brown); a tint you draw yourself to sit beside a chart is `withOpacity(color, chartFill)` from `@/theme`, never a hex suffix. There is no ring: amounts are shown with `Vessel`, `MiniCup`, `Trough` or `ProgressBar`.
- Feedback: `toast(message, { label, onPress, tint })`, `haptic.tap/press/select/success/warn`. The toast is an ink cup with a level line along its top in `tint` (the data colour of what was logged, as a theme colour name such as `'water'`, which also works from code outside React, or a colour value; the primary by default). It rests 12 px above the bottom chrome of the screen that is showing (the tab bar, a `Screen` footer or the home indicator; `Screen` reports it with `useToastDock` while it is focused, so a toast raised on one screen moves above the next one's tab bar the moment it shows), never mid-screen and never over the tab bar or a footer. While the on-screen keyboard is up it rests 12 px above the footer that rides on the keyboard, or 12 px above the keyboard when the screen has no footer (the tab bar and home indicator are behind it then), and moves above the focused field when it would cover it and there is room; the keyboard is measured as `Screen` pads for it (KeyboardAvoidingView's padding natively, `keyboardCover` in a phone browser) and the rule is `toastBottom` (`src/ui/keyboardInset.ts`). A `Sheet` shows toasts itself, just above its top edge, so there is one `toast-action` on screen at a time. Its action takes no press in its first 600 ms on screen (`TOAST_ACTION_GUARD_MS` in `src/ui/Toast.tsx`: stamped when the toast first shows, the clock read in the press handler), so a quick second tap meant for the button it rose beside (Today's "+ 250 ml") never undoes the first. A script that taps `toast-action` waits with `toastActionReady` from `scripts/dev/toastGuard.cjs` (e2e.cjs, crawl.cjs, health-export-e2e.cjs and evidence.cjs's state runner already do), never with sleeps of its own; e2e's 'toast guard' check proves it (a tap at about 100 ms does nothing, one at 700 ms undoes). A toast leaves by fading and sinking over 160 ms (`motion.duration.fade`), also when its action is pressed, and takes no second press while it leaves; a toast the action raises stays, and a toast raised while another is leaving rises back from where that fade had reached and is never cleared by it (`closeToast` in `src/ui/Toast.tsx`).
- Waiting: `PourWait` (`@/components/PourWait`; `size` 'tile' 40 x 52 or 'inline' 16 x 20, optional `label`) is the waiting cup for every wait that is not the AI (signing in, a reset link being checked, the first sync after a sign-in, a payment arriving): a plain cup whose level rises, rests and fades in a loop, half full and still under reduced motion, always beside a sentence that says what is happening. A waiting screen draws `WaitLines` (same file: the cup, a strong line that says what is happening, a secondary line whose last two words stay together). Never a bare `ActivityIndicator` on a screen of its own, and never `AiMark`, which means the AI is working: the cup with the drop is the AI's, the plain cup is waiting, and the two are never swapped.
- Icons: `lucide-react-native`, stroke 1.9 (2.2 when active or on a button), 18 to 22 px, colour from the theme, with two families of the app's own. The AI's, from `@/features/ai/mark`: `AiMark` (the AI's cup with its drop above the open top: `size` 'hero', 'row' or 'small', `tall` for the coach's larger hero, `working` pours it in a loop while the AI works) and `AiGlyph` ('estimate', 'photo', 'describe', 'coach': the same cup as line glyphs on lucide's grid and stroke, in a theme colour passed in; 'photo' is a camera whose lens is the drop, and corner frames belong to the barcode only). Wherever the AI made something or is working (the estimate's wait and its review, an AI coach answer, the coach's waiting row) use `AiMark`; on a button, a tile or a row that leads to the AI or names its allowance use `AiGlyph`. Lucide's `Sparkles`, `Wand`, `Bot` and `Brain` are not used for the AI, nor is a sparkle emoji, and the AI is never drawn any other way. An AI-estimated food's data-source mark is `SourceMark` (the small `AiMark`; the kit's `CupKey` for every other source). The sample result is not the AI: its head wears the flask, and its header's short line and its review rows say "Sample", never "AI estimate" (`SourceTag` with `plain` and `label`, the kit's dashed key). `AiMark`'s box is the cup plus the drop's room (`AI_MARK_BOX`); to stand the cup where a plain cup stood, pull it up by `aiMarkHeadroom(size)`, as `CoachMark` does. The only other glyph of the app's own is the plan's, `PlanGlyph` (`src/features/plan/PlanGlyph.tsx`: the open cup filled to 60% with its level line inside the walls). A data icon takes its data colour only when it stands alone; next to a number use a `CupKey`.
- Motion (three verbs: pour, settle, tick; tokens in `motion` from `@/theme`, see `src/theme/motion.ts`):
  - `useReducedMotion()` is the one switch for the whole app (the system setting, prefers-reduced-motion on web, and any automated browser unless the page sets `window.__healthcalMotion = true` before loading). Every kit animation already obeys it; anything you animate yourself must too (`isReducedMotion()` outside components).
  - `Vessel`, `MiniCup`, `Trough` and `ProgressBar` pour on mount and on change and the rim settles. A pour that starts as its cup mounts waits until the page draws evenly (two frames in a row under 40 ms apart, 600 ms at most; one shared watcher, so cups still start together with their own stagger), so its first step is never half its height. What appears as a pour lands (the brim line and its one flash, the amber cap, the hatched headroom) comes when the level is within 5% of the cup of where it stops (`pourLandsAt` in `@/theme/motion`, through `Pour.endsIn`), not at the pour's last millisecond. A new pour eases a running rim settle back to 0 first, so the level line never rides below the fill's top. `delay`, `duration`, `animate`, and `memoryKey` (a stable key such as `cal-${date}`, so a remount with the same level does not pour again). `MiniCup` takes `index` for a left-to-right stagger in a row. With `goalKind="reach"`, crossing the goal on screen makes the rim flash once in the data colour (`useReachFlash`; skipped under reduced motion and when Celebrations is off in Profile).
  - `AnimatedNumber`: a `Text` whose number ticks to each new value (`value`, `format`, `decimals`, `from` to count up on mount, `memoryKey`; children go after the number, e.g. the unit).
  - `FadeUp` (`index` for a sequence): content fading up 8 px on mount. `Screen` does this for every screen (`enter={false}` to opt out); on web it is the page change.
  - `AnimatedList` + `AnimatedItem` (keyed by the row's id): rows slide in, fade out and the rest move smoothly. Key the list by the day it shows. On the web a new row only fades in (Reanimated's predefined `FadeIn`): never give a row a custom `Keyframe` as `entering` on the web, because Reanimated's web clean-up pins it with position: absolute a little later and the row leaves the layout (a coach question under its answer, a diary row over the next one). e2e's 'lists with motion on' block checks the coach, the diary and Water with motion on. The diary's and Water's log rows (`FadeRow` and `useLeavingRows` in `@/features/food/rowMotion`) fade out in place, then leave, and the rows below close the space in one step with the card around them: no row glides, because a row gliding inside a card that has already shrunk is drawn past its bottom edge over the next card.
  - `AiMark working` is the one loop for the AI at work (1,120 ms a turn, `startWaitLoop` in `@/features/ai/mark/loop`: the drop falls in as the level pours up to `WAIT_HIGH`, 0.8, never the brim, so the level line always shows; the level falls back and the drop returns). The level line that rises over the meal photo while an estimate is on its way (`PourVeil` in `src/features/ai/AnalysingView.tsx`) is the one loop over a photo: it starts at the bottom as the veil fades in, fades out near the top, and has a 1 px card-coloured edge either side so it shows on any photo. Both are indeterminate and never claim a share done; under reduced motion both are still.
  - Charts pour their columns and draw their line once on mount; `Sheet` slides up once this opening has been measured (120 ms at most) over `motion.duration.sheetIn` (300 ms) on `easing.arrive`, which moves from its first frame and slows to rest, with a fading backdrop, and a toast it shows fades with it when it closes, then rises over the tab bar; tabs cross-fade; task screens (food search, quick add, scan, photo, import, add friend) rise from the bottom.
  - Only transforms and opacity animate. The stored value changes at once; never wait for an animation in code or tests. Screenshots (`shot.mjs`) and e2e runs are reduced motion; `shot.mjs --motion --frames=8` captures an animation frame by frame.

Read the component source in `src/ui/` for exact props before using one. If the kit lacks something you need, build it inside your own `src/features/<area>/` folder; do not edit `src/ui/`, `src/theme/`, `src/store/`, `src/lib/` or `src/types/` unless your brief says you own the file. Report gaps instead.

## Design rules

- Never hard-code a colour, font or radius. Use `useTheme()` (`colors`, `spacing`, `radius`) and the helpers from `@/theme` (`cup`, `cupFor`, `fonts`). There are six themes (dark: a warm black with cream text, no brown in the surfaces; light oat paper; the pink Blossom theme; and three from the owner's palette: Butter, butter paper with a raspberry cup; Honey, butter paper with a gold cup; Java, a dark coffee brown with butter ink) and all must look right, so never branch on `dark` to pick a colour: add a token instead.
- Flat only: no gradients, no glow, no blur, no shadows (the Sheet is the one exception). Everything is a flat fill plus one line.
- The level line is the signature: a 3 px `colors.rim` bar on top of every fill (2.5 px in mini cups and bar charts), the tick on every progress bar, the active tab and the selected segment. `rim` is never a data colour.
- Empty reads as empty. The part of a cup that is not filled is `colors.vesselEmpty` (equal to the card colour), outlined in `colors.vesselEdge`: never `track`, never a tint that could be taken for content. `colors.track` is for the unfilled part of a progress bar only. A cup drawn by hand follows the same rule and has no lip. Hatched exercise headroom stays visible as hatching.
- Reaching a goal. A goal that is meant to be reached (water, activity minutes, protein, a habit) is `goalKind="reach"` on `Vessel`, `MiniCup`, `Trough`, `ProgressBar` and `BarChart`; a budget (calories) is the default `'limit'`. Pass the real value, not one capped at the goal. At or past a reach goal the cup is brim-full and the level line becomes the brim line: it lies across the open top, 3 to 6 px wider than the cup on each side and 1 to 2 px heavier (on a bar or trough, a taller, heavier tick at the end). That is the whole reached state: no amber, no badge, no check mark on the cup, no confetti. When the level crosses the goal while the cup is on screen the brim line spreads into place and flashes once in the data colour; a cup that mounts already full, or again with the same `memoryKey`, does not. `onReached` fires once at that crossing (in the same tick as the change, also under reduced motion), so the screen adds `haptic.success()` there, and a line of neutral text never before its number reads the goal (Water's "Goal reached for today." comes with the brim line, or as the count first reads 100% when that is later: `reachedWordsAfter` in `src/app/water.tsx`; at once under reduced motion). Two things to know: a cup that can show more than one day needs a `memoryKey` with the day in it (a new key is a mount, so paging onto a day that met its goal marks nothing), and every mounted cup that sees the crossing calls its `onReached`, so a tab screen that stays mounted under another screen should react only while it is focused.
- Containers are cups: square shoulders on top, a round base. Spread `cup(radius.card)` on cards (6 6 22 22), `cup(radius.button)` on buttons, `cup(radius.buttonSm, 5)` on small buttons, `cup(radius.tile, 5)` or `cupFor(size)` on tiles, avatars and icon buttons. Labels (chips, segments, keys, badges) keep small equal corners (`radius.chip`, 6). Nothing is a pill, nothing is fully round, and no large uniform radius. `radius.pill` is 6 now; do not use it in new code.
- Semantic colours are fixed: calories `colors.calories`, protein `colors.protein`, carbs `colors.carbs`, fat `colors.fat`, water `colors.water`, exercise `colors.exercise`, sleep `colors.sleep`, weight `colors.weight`. Data colours are fills and key marks only, never text. Calorie numbers use `colors.caloriesInk`; other numbers use `text`. Exercise-added budget is hatched in `colors.headroom`; vessel outlines are `colors.vesselEdge`.
- Over target is `colors.warning` (an amber cap across a cup's own width, the over part of a bar under a level line 1 px heavier at the bar's own width: an over mark never reaches past its walls, because a line wider than its cup or bar is the brim, which means a goal reached), with neutral words, and never shown by colour alone. Never red, and never for a goal that was reached. `warning` is a fill or a key mark only: warning text, and a warning icon beside text, use `colors.warningInk`.
- Status colours: `colors.success` is ink (equal to `text`), for done marks and checks; there is no status green, because it could not be told from exercise or carbs. `colors.danger` is a red of its own, at least 15 ΔE from the primary in every theme, for destructive actions and field errors.
- Contrast: 4.5:1 for small text, 3:1 for large numbers, for the level line against the empty part of a cup, for the cup outline and for a data fill on a card. `textTertiary` goes on `bg`, `surface` and the tab bar, never on `surfaceAlt`. `primary` as text goes only on `surface` (light primary on `bg` is 4.48); anywhere else (on `bg`, `surfaceAlt`, the soft button's fill) use `colors.primaryInk`. These rules are a test: `src/theme/contrast.test.ts` computes every pair for all six palettes and fails when a palette change breaks one (its last block records the one thing that cannot hold for Dark and Blossom: the level line at 3:1 on top of a fill, which only the light palette reaches). Butter, Honey and Java are held to the full rule with no exception: every data fill and the amber 3:1 on the card, the level line 3:1 on every data fill, on the amber and on the empty cup, and the over mark (`warning`) well apart from the calorie fill. Dark's over mark is held as well: `warning` at least 25 apart (CIEDE2000) from the calorie fill, and 22 apart as bars at Dark's chart opacity.
- One primary action per screen, pinned in the `Screen` footer when the screen is a form.
- The top of a screen. There are two headers and every screen uses one of them, passed as `Screen`'s `header` so it stays put while the page scrolls. Tab screens (Today, Diary, Train, Progress) use `TabHeader` (`@/components/TabHeader`): an overline (the date, the week, the range) over a one-line title, and on the right always Coach, Friends, then the profile tile (which opens Profile, where settings live): three 36 px cups 4 px apart, each 40 px wide and 44 px tall to touch (`HEADER_SLOP`: at 40 px from centre to centre a 44 px wide area would overlap its neighbour's; this is the one stated exception to the 44 px target, and the scan in `evidence.cjs` knows it by the `tab-header` testID), the same on all four, with their own testIDs (`today-coach-open`, `today-friends`, `diary-profile`, ...). The Coach cup is the one accented cup (a `primarySoft` fill and a `primaryInk` speech bubble; its unread level line on that tint is held to 3:1 in `contrast.test.ts`); Friends and the profile tile stay neutral. Nothing else goes in it: a screen's own controls (Today's share, the diary's day arrows, Progress's range) sit in its content under the header. Today's title is its greeting, chosen by `todayTitle` (`@/features/today/greetingTitle`): the name is kept first, 'Good evening, Dana' then 'Evening, Dana' at the tab's 26 px, then the same two stepped to 24 px (`TITLE_STEP`; the line height and the 68 px header do not change), and only then 'Good evening' or 'Evening' without the name; at 360 px every greeting keeps a 4-letter name ('Good night, Alex' at 24 px), and at 320 px the title stays 22 px with no step. The tab passes the size as `titleSize`. Under 360 px an overline that does not fit takes its short form (`overlineShort`): only Progress's early state needs one ('Trends'); the longest day, 'Wednesday, Nov 18', fits at 320 (`tabDay(date, true)`, 'Wed, Nov 18', is there if a longer one ever does not). The Coach cup (`CoachEntry`, `@/features/coach/CoachEntry`) shows a short level line inside its speech bubble while an answer arrived with the coach closed (`unread` in `useCoachSession`, memory only); never a number. Pushed screens use the kit's `Header`: a back cup on the left, a title, always one short line under it (where you are or what it is for: "Today", "Goal 74 kg", "Settings and goals"; a few words, never a sentence), so every title sits at the same height, and at most one action on the right, an `IconButton` cup (share, delete, favourite), never a text link. The screens that rise from the bottom as tasks (`TASK_SCREENS` in `src/app/_layout.tsx`: food search, quick add, scan, photo or describe, Garmin import, add a friend, the morning page, the coach) close with the X (`close`); every other pushed screen goes back with the chevron. The header's title is the screen's title: never a second large title under it, in any state of the screen (one stated exception: the coach's hero, `coach-hello`, a greeting by name over the coach's mark centred in an empty chat, because there it is the coach speaking, not a title for the screen; evidence.cjs and tops.cjs know it by its testID) (a review step, a done step, an error, an empty state): what changes between steps goes in the short line ("Two things to check", "1 workout added"), and a heading in the content is at most a section title's size (`h3`, 18 px) unless it sits inside a card (a food's name, the recap's win of the week). The first-run flow (welcome, onboarding with its progress header, the sign-in gate's waiting screens) is the one exception and keeps its large question titles. Both headers start 8 px below the safe area and leave 16 px to the first content (the scroll area's 8 px top padding plus the header's own 8 px); keep it that way by not adding top margins or padding to a screen's first block. `node scripts/dev/tops.cjs <dir>` shows the top of every screen side by side and prints these numbers.
- Numbers that matter are large Bricolage (`numL`/`numXL`), their unit small (`unit` variant) beside them. Group related stats in one card, separated by dividers, rather than many small cards.
- Emoji are content marks only: 17 px, no tile, in food and activity rows. Never in headers, buttons, navigation or empty states. People get initials in a cup tile.
- Spacing comes from the scale (4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32). Screen side padding is 20 (handled by `Screen`). Card padding 16 to 18.
- Room: two pieces of text are never closer than 8 px (between their letters) unless they are one sentence, a number with its unit, or a title with its one line under it; blocks that are different things (a label and the next row, a row and the next card's content) are 12 px or more apart. Anything placed by a value (a label at a cup's level, a tick, a chart label) keeps that room at every value, not only the demo's.
- 360 px first: every screen must work at 360 x 740 (the most common Android phone, the owner's), as well as at 390 and 320; what the person came to do is in the first view at 360 x 740.
- Touch targets are at least 44 px (small buttons and chips are 32 to 36 visible with `hitSlop`, which the kit turns into a real hit area on web too: on anything built on `PressableScale`, pass `hitSlop={(44 - size) / 2}`). Every interactive element has an `accessibilityLabel` (the kit adds one from `title`/`label` where it can) and a stable `testID` in kebab-case (`food-search-input`, `log-activity-save`).
- Content is capped at 560 px wide and centred (handled by `Screen`), so the app reads well on tablets and desktop web.
- Empty states explain what the screen will show and offer the action that fills it.
- Sentence case everywhere. No exclamation marks in routine copy. Units always shown.

## Platform rules

- The same code runs on iOS, Android and web. Guard native-only APIs (camera, notifications, haptics, file system, sharing) with `Platform.OS` and give web a working fallback.
- `Alert.alert` does not work on web. Use `ConfirmSheet` or `toast`.
- Never nest one pressable inside another (for example a `Card` with `onPress` that contains a `Button` or `IconButton`). On web both render as `<button>`, which is invalid HTML and breaks keyboard and screen-reader use. Make them siblings: a row `View` holding the main `PressableScale` and the secondary button side by side.
- Navigation: `router.push('/path?x=1')`, `useLocalSearchParams()`. Leave a screen with `goBack()` from `@/ui` so a deep-linked page still has somewhere to go.
- Over-the-air updates live in `src/features/updates`: the rules (`rules.ts`: check at most every 4 hours on return to the front; start a downloaded update only on return after five minutes or more away (a new calendar day alone is not enough), on a tab, with no sheet open, no sync running, nothing written since the return, everything saved, never the same update twice and at most once every 6 hours) are pure and tested with `fake.ts`; `platform.native.ts` is the only file that loads `expo-updates` (it answers null on web, in a development build and when the module is missing, and every failure is a `console.warn`); About reads `useUpdatesStatus`. Never call `expo-updates` anywhere else. `expo.runtimeVersion` in `app.json` is a fixed string: when you add or upgrade a package with native code or change a native setting in `app.json`, raise it, have both apps rebuilt, and record the new pair with `HEALTHCAL_RECORD_NATIVE=1 npx vitest run src/features/updates/nativeSurface.test.ts`; that test fails until then (it hashes the native packages, every `app.json` key but `web`, `extra`, `owner`, `slug`, `version` and `experiments`, the local config plugins' source and `.eas/build`; recording a new hash under the same runtimeVersion is refused unless `HEALTHCAL_RECORD_SAME_RUNTIME=1`, only for a change to the hash itself). Publish with `EXPO_TOKEN=... node scripts/ops/publish-update.mjs --message "..."` (`--dry-run` first): it exports the bundles itself with only `eas.json`'s production `EXPO_PUBLIC_*` values, refuses bundles that hold the test hooks, and uploads them with `eas update --skip-bundler`; browser states for About are `--states=updates` (`?updatesDemo=<preset>`, test builds only).

## Working on this project

- There is no standing dev server. Run every browser check against a fresh build that is served only while the check runs: `node scripts/dev/with-build.cjs [--dir=<folder>] [--port=8200] [--reuse] -- <command> --url={url}` exports the web app (with the test hooks, no backend), serves it for exactly as long as the command runs and exits with its exit code. `--reuse` skips the export when the code has not changed since the last one; two runs at once need their own `--dir` and `--port`. Every script below takes `--url`. Do not start `expo start` or any other server that outlives your command.
- A command you run in the foreground ends after 10 minutes: split a long scan into pieces that finish within 8 (one list, one width, or `--only`), and never end your work while a scan you need is still running.
- Look at your screens in a real browser: `node scripts/dev/shot.mjs <route> <out.png> --url=<the build's url>` loads demo data, opens the route at 360 x 740 (`--width=390` or `320` for the others) and saves a screenshot, printing any runtime errors. Flags: `--empty` (profile but no data), `--fresh` (no profile), `--stress`, `--light`, `--theme=blossom|butter|honey|java`, `--imperial`, `--values=<json>`, `--full`, `--2x`, `--tap=testIdA,testIdB`, `--type=testId:text`, `--wait=ms`, `--desktop`. Open the PNG with the Read tool and judge it critically: alignment, spacing, truncation, contrast, both themes, empty and filled states.
- Type-check with `npx tsc --noEmit`. Other people are editing other files at the same time, so errors in files you do not own are not yours to fix; if the bundle is broken by someone else's file, wait a minute and retry.
- The code map: `docs/code-map/` holds a graph of every TypeScript file under `src/` and `supabase/functions/`, as plain text in git (`exports.tsv` every exported name with its kind, `uses.tsv` who imports each name, through re-exports to the file that defines it, `imports.tsv` what each file depends on; the README says how each case is read). Look there first for who uses a name or what a file depends on, with grep, before reading files: `grep -P '\thasFoodLogged\t' docs/code-map/uses.tsv`, `grep -P '^src/app/water.tsx\t' docs/code-map/imports.tsv`, and for what breaks when a file changes `grep -P '\tsrc/lib/hydration.ts(\t|$)' docs/code-map/imports.tsv | cut -f1`. For everything that depends on a file through other files, screens first: `node scripts/dev/code-map.mjs --dependents=src/features/morning/rule.ts`. After adding, removing or renaming an export, changing what a file imports, or adding, moving or deleting a file, refresh it with `node scripts/dev/code-map.mjs` (about 2 s); `src/codeMap.test.ts` fails until you do. An edit inside a function or a comment changes nothing in it.
- Unit tests: `npx vitest run` (pure logic only, files named `*.test.ts`). `react-native`, AsyncStorage and `expo-crypto` are stubbed for tests (see `test/stubs/`).
- Browser tests read the app's live state with `await page.evaluate(() => JSON.parse(JSON.stringify(window.__healthcal.getState())))`. Do not parse `localStorage` yourself: the store is saved as many small values (`src/store/persistence.ts`), not one.
- `node scripts/dev/e2e.cjs` runs a new user's first day end to end; keep it passing.
- `node scripts/dev/evidence.cjs --out=<dir> --routes=/,/diary` gathers the facts about a set of screens with no judgement: every theme asked for (`--themes`, blossom, light and dark by default; butter, honey and java on request) at 360, 390 and 320 px (360 first), runtime errors, text that is cut off, pages wider than the screen and touch targets under 44 px, and the layout of the whole page: text drawn over text or running into the next text, a word split over two lines, a number split from its unit ("68 fl" / "oz"), an overline that leaves its last word alone on a second line, a wrapping row of chips that leaves one alone on its last line, text or a box reaching past the card or row that holds it, controls that overlap, content left under the tab bar or a footer at the end of the page, cards closer than 8 px, text closer than 4 px to its card's edge, text running into the screen's side gutter, cards with uneven side margins, a header that breaks the rule above (no short line, a second large title under it) and a toast over the tab bar, a footer or an open sheet's controls (judged at the real window height). Names that end in an ellipsis after their allowed lines are listed apart as "shortened by design"; `cut` is text clipped with no ellipsis, including a placeholder wider than its field, a number field's value wider than the field and a clamped name whose box shows lines after its ellipsis. It writes `report.json`, `<id>__<theme>__<width>.png` (the first view) and, when the page scrolls, `<id>__<theme>__<width>__full.png` (the whole page, as the layout scan judged it: look there for a layout finding), and one sheet per screen (`--empty`, `--fresh`, `--checks` to also run the test suite, `--show=100` to print more screens). Run it before asking anyone to review a screen; the header comment says what it leaves out on purpose, and `node scripts/dev/evidence-selftest.cjs` proves every scan (and every exception) against a hand-made fault: add a case there when you change a scan.
- `tight` (in evidence.cjs) reports two pieces of text from different blocks closer than 8 px one above the other, or 6 px side by side, measured between the letters (not the line boxes), with the gap; it leaves alone the lines of one paragraph, a number and its unit (nested, or two blocks with the unit set smaller), a component's stacked lines (title and subtitle, stat and caption), a label over its value or field, unless the text is placed by a value (absolutely positioned, like a cup's labels). It also reports text less than 6 px above or below a box it is not in (a button, a badge, a field, a dashed slot, a card), except a label over its own field and a caption under a picture. Text in the tab bar, a pinned header or footer is never judged against the page that scrolls under it.
- One route, one state or one list at the three widths: `node scripts/dev/with-build.cjs --dir=<folder> --port=<port> -- node scripts/dev/evidence.cjs --url={url} --themes=blossom --out=<dir> --routes=/water` (or `--states=move --only=water-reached`, or `--states=food`); add `--reuse` while the code under src has not changed since the last export. Everything lands in `<dir>`: `report.json`, the PNGs and `sheet-<id>.png`. A whole list takes several minutes per width, so a long one runs as one width per command (`--widths=360`, then 390, then 320, each in its own `--out`) to finish inside a foreground command's 10 minutes.
- Values: a state's `values` (`{ eaten, protein, exercise, water, weekMinutes, fasting, eatBack, spread }`: shares of today's budget, protein target and water goal, net exercise kcal, guideline minutes this week, share of the fasting target, `eatBack: 0` to leave exercise out of the budget, and `spread: 1` to put the demo on "Over 7 days" (the demo itself is on "Same day"), `drinks: 1|2` for one or two drinks with calories logged on Water today, `diaryDrink: 1` for a glass of milk logged in the diary only at 08:05, `favoriteMeal: 1` to star the saved meal listed last, `savedEstimate: 1` for "Chicken and rice bowl" saved from an AI estimate with ranges, `2` to star it too) sets today's amounts on the demo data through `buildSeed({ values })` (`seed.mjs`, using the app's own budget maths); `shot.mjs --values='{"eaten":0.6}' --width=360` does the same for one screenshot.
- Sweeps: `--states=sweep` (`scripts/dev/states/sweep.cjs`) takes every labelled cup and bar (Today's calorie cup, the diary's summary, water, Train's week, fasting, the share card, the plan reveal) at the values that stress it; run it whenever you change one of them.
- States: `--states=<list>` runs `scripts/dev/states/<list>.cjs`, an array of `{ id, route, data: demo|empty|fresh|stress, imperial, day, values, now, flags, type, taps, waitFor, absent, sheet }` (format in the header of `evidence.cjs`; `sample.cjs` has one of each kind), so open sheets, other sections, first-run steps and edit modes are captured and scanned like routes; `--only=<id>` runs one. The states that need accounts (sign-in, the account screen, signed-in friends) are listed only with `--backend`, against a build with the stand-in backend. A capture that is blank, half-loaded or whose tap failed is retried, then reported as NOT CAPTURED, never clean.
- Accounts as pictures: a test-hook build reads `?authDemo=<view>` (`src/features/auth/demo.ts`: google, signin, signup, declined, waiting, checking, restoring, inbox, signed-in, signed-out) and draws welcome, the sign-in screen and Account as with accounts, with no backend; beside `signed-in`, `&syncDemo=synced|waiting|syncing|paused|offline` draws the sync row in that state and `&demoEmail=long` a 55-letter address. Every one of them reads the address through one gate, which answers nothing in a release bundle (`demo.test.ts`). The states are under 'accounts as pictures' in `scripts/dev/states/start.cjs` (`welcome-google-demo`, `welcome-google-declined`, `auth-demo-*`, `account-demo-*`, `account-demo-sync-*`; with `&planDemo=plus` Account shows its Plan group and the delete sheet with a plan). The crawl leaves them out, since their buttons have no backend behind them; what those views do is proved against the stand-in backend (`--backend`, `e2e-backend.cjs`).
- With a sheet open only the sheet is scanned, plus its top above the screen, content cut by the bottom, its last control under 12 px from the bottom, a body that almost fits (it scrolls by 80 px or less, so a line shows half cut at its bottom edge), content inside its side padding and a toast over a control; `node scripts/dev/evidence-selftest.cjs` proves every scan against hand-made faults.
- Long content: `buildSeed({ stress: true })` (`shot.mjs --stress`, `data: 'stress'`, a fourth mode in `check-routes.cjs`) is the same five weeks with a 22-character name, 50 to 60 character food names, a 13-item meal, own foods and a saved meal named in Hebrew, 11,480 and 0 kcal days, 12.5 L of water, 142.6 kg and a 412-day streak.
- `node scripts/dev/tops.cjs <dir> [--theme=blossom|light|dark|butter|honey|java] [--width=360,390,320]` crops the top of every screen into one sheet (`<dir>/_sheet.png`) and prints, per screen, where the header starts, its height, the gap to the content under it, where the title sits and how many lines the title block has. The four tabs must match each other, and so must the pushed screens.
- `node scripts/dev/check-routes.cjs` opens every route with nothing stored, with an empty profile and with demo data, and fails on any error screen. Add a new route to its list. A screen opened before setup is sent to `/welcome` by one rule in `src/app/_layout.tsx` (`OPEN_WITHOUT_PROFILE`); a new screen that must work without a profile is added there.
- `node scripts/dev/crawl.cjs [--only=<route or state id>] [--url=...] [--hosted=<page.html>]` taps every visible control on every route and every state in `scripts/dev/states/{start,food,move}.cjs` with a finger in a 360 x 740 phone browser, each from a fresh load, and reports what each did (another screen, a sheet, a toast, stored data, the page, a hand-off to the system, or nothing), checks the back or close control and the browser's back return to where the person was, and fails on controls that do nothing, moves that end on an error, returns that do not return and controls that need two taps. `--hosted` crawls the hosted preview's single page inside a sandboxed frame, where downloads, file pickers, the camera and links out are blocked; such controls must say so (`@/services/viewer`). Before the taps it proves the gestures with real touch events (`--gestures-only` runs just those): that the day never changes by accident (a slow drag changes nothing on Today or the diary, Today has no page swipe, the diary's flick moves a day with a toast whose "Today" returns and never goes into the future) and that both show today again after time away, the week strip, and every kind of sheet following the finger, settling, closing, still scrolling, under reduced motion, and with a mouse and Escape on a desktop-size window. A full run takes about an hour: run it in the background, in pieces (`--routes-only`, then `--states-only --lists=<one> --no-gestures`).
