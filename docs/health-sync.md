# Automatic workout sync, daily steps and sleep (Health Connect on Android, Apple Health on iPhone)

Garmin Connect, and other apps such as Samsung Health, Strava or Polar Flow, can write each workout to Android's **Health Connect** or to **Apple Health** on an iPhone (where an Apple Watch writes its workouts by itself). With one permission, HealthCal reads them from there and adds them to Train by themselves, with the calories the watch measured. No file export is needed. It also reads the day's **steps** and shows them on Train; steps are never added to the calorie budget (see "Steps"). And it reads **sleep**: each night becomes a record in the sleep log, one per wake day, marked with where it came from (see "Sleep"). Since the round of 18 November 2026 it also reads **weigh-ins** with body fat into Weight (see "Weight and body fat"), the watch's own **calories burned in a day**, shown on Progress beside HealthCal's estimate and added to nothing (see "The day's calories burned"), and the **average and highest heart rate** of a synced workout, shown on that workout (see "Heart rate in a workout"). None of these three has run on a phone yet (see "Not yet proven: weight, the day's calories and heart rate").

Open it from **Profile > Workout sync** or **Train > Sync automatically** (route `/settings/health-sync`). The screen names Apple Health on an iPhone and Health Connect on Android. In the browser (and on an iPad without Apple Health) it says that automatic sync is in the iPhone and Android apps (`reach.ts`), that an iPhone copy installed with a sideloading tool may be signed without Apple's Health permission and that its Workout sync screen says so, and offers one way forward: **Import your Health export** (the Health app's own export file, see "The Health app's export file"), with the Garmin file import (`docs/garmin-import.md`) as a link under it; the browser never loads either native module. An iPhone copy signed without the HealthKit entitlement says so on the sync screen itself and offers the same import (see "Installing on an iPhone with a free Apple ID").

The person has to switch sharing on in Garmin Connect too (Garmin Connect > settings > Health Connect, or > Connected apps > Apple Health on an iPhone). Only workouts recorded on a Garmin device are shared, according to Garmin's support pages as reported by the press.

**State of the iPhone side.** The native library is installed and bound: `@kingstinct/react-native-healthkit` 16.2.0 (with `@react-native-healthkit/core` 16.2.0) and `react-native-nitro-modules` 0.37.2, its config plugin in `app.json` with the read text only, and `adapter.ios.ts` as the binding (see "How the iPhone side is bound"). So the Profile row, the sync screen and the privacy paragraph on About name Apple Health: they read one flag, `APPLE_HEALTH_IN_APP` in `src/features/health-sync/reach.ts`, now `true`, and `reach.test.ts` fails when that flag and `adapter.ios.ts` disagree. To read Apple Health, a person needs an iPhone build signed with the HealthKit entitlement: one signed through the paid Apple Developer Program (TestFlight, the App Store, or EAS internal distribution with a registered device), or one built with Xcode on a Mac with the person's own free account. A copy sideloaded with AltStore, SideStore or Sideloadly is signed without it and shows "Apple Health is not available in this install"; Impactor and xtool may keep it, which is not yet tried on a phone (see "Installing on an iPhone with a free Apple ID"). Checked: `npx tsc --noEmit`, the health-sync unit tests and `npx expo-doctor` (20 of 20) pass; the iPhone project that `npx expo prebuild --platform ios` generates has the `com.apple.developer.healthkit` entitlement and no background-delivery entitlement, `NSHealthShareUsageDescription` with the app's text and no `NSHealthUpdateUsageDescription`; the exported iPhone JavaScript bundle contains the HealthKit module, and the Android and web bundles contain neither it nor Nitro Modules' JavaScript. The Android app builds with `react-native-nitro-modules` autolinked and `expo-updates` (Gradle on this machine, x86_64, and on a GitHub runner). The iPhone app compiles with the library on Expo's Mac and on GitHub's, and on an iPhone simulator it shows Apple's access sheet and reads typed steps (see "Proven on an iPhone simulator"). Not checked yet: anything on a real iPhone, and reading a workout from Apple Health (see "Not yet proven on iPhone"). On an Android emulator Health Connect's permission screen is answered and a sync runs against an empty store (see "Proven on an Android emulator"); steps and workouts from Health Connect have not yet been read from a store that holds any (see "Not yet proven on Android").

## What is read

### Android: Health Connect

Nine read permissions, declared in `app.json` (`android.permissions`) and requested by `READ_PERMISSIONS` in `src/features/health-sync/healthConnect.ts`. Turning sync on asks for all nine; the three added after sleep are also asked row by row (`WEIGHT_PERMISSIONS`, `DAY_ENERGY_PERMISSIONS`, `HEART_PERMISSIONS`), so a phone that allowed the first six sees only that row's types on that row's "Allow":

| Permission | Used for |
|---|---|
| `READ_EXERCISE` | The workout: its type, start and end, zone offset, title, the app that wrote it and the device name. |
| `READ_ACTIVE_CALORIES_BURNED` | Energy above the device's resting rate inside the workout. Used when the workout has no total (see Calories). Also the day's active total, only to tell which days a watch recorded (see "The day's calories burned"). |
| `READ_TOTAL_CALORIES_BURNED` | Energy including resting inside the workout: the session's total, from which the net figure is worked out exactly as for a Garmin file. Also one total per day, shown on Progress beside HealthCal's estimate and never added to anything (see "The day's calories burned"). The Calories burned row's "Allow" asks these two again (`DAY_ENERGY_PERMISSIONS`). |
| `READ_DISTANCE` | Distance inside the workout. |
| `READ_STEPS` | Steps per day for Train, as Health Connect totals them (see "Steps"). Added after the first four: a phone that allowed those four is asked for steps only when the person taps "Allow" on the sync screen's steps row. |
| `READ_SLEEP` | Sleep sessions (`SleepSession`) with their stages, for the sleep log (see "Sleep"). Added after steps: a phone that allowed the first five is asked only when the person taps "Allow" on the sync screen's Sleep row. `app.json` must list `android.permission.health.READ_SLEEP` (the lead adds it with the runtime version). |
| `READ_WEIGHT` | Weigh-ins (`Weight`: `weight.inKilograms`, `time`, `zoneOffset`, `metadata.dataOrigin`), one a day, into Weight (see "Weight and body fat"). Added after sleep: a phone that allowed the six is asked only on the Weight row's "Allow". |
| `READ_BODY_FAT` | Body fat (`BodyFat`: `percentage`, 0 to 100), saved with that day's weigh-in when the same app measured it within 10 minutes. Added after sleep, asked with weight on the Weight row's "Allow". |
| `READ_HEART_RATE` | Heart rate only inside a workout, from the workout's own app (`aggregateRecord` with `BPM_AVG`, `BPM_MAX`, `MEASUREMENTS_COUNT`): its average and highest, shown on the workout (see "Heart rate in a workout"). Added after sleep: a phone that allowed the six is asked only on the "Heart rate in workouts" row's "Allow". |

Nothing else: no resting heart rate, heart-rate variability, location or routes, no writing, no background reading and no `READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY` (so Health Connect gives at most the 30 days before the permission was first granted). `app.json` must list the three new permissions (`android.permission.health.READ_WEIGHT`, `READ_BODY_FAT`, `READ_HEART_RATE`); the lead adds them with a new runtime version. The person can allow only some of the nine: with exercise alone, workouts are added with the app's own calorie estimate.

Energy and distance are read **only inside each workout's own interval and only from the app that wrote that workout** (`aggregateRecord` with the session's time range and `dataOriginFilter`). All-day energy is read only as one total per day for Progress, never added to a workout or the budget. An aggregate whose `dataOrigins` does not include the workout's own app is ignored, and so is a total on its own that works out below 1.5 MET (Health Connect can answer a total-energy question with a resting estimate).

### iPhone: Apple Health (HealthKit)

Five read types, asked for by `READ_TYPES` in `src/features/health-sync/healthKit.ts` with `requestAuthorization({ toRead })`, and four more kept apart in their own lists: sleep in `SLEEP_READ_TYPES`, body mass and body fat in `WEIGHT_READ_TYPES`, resting energy in `ENERGY_READ_TYPES` and heart rate in `HEART_READ_TYPES`. Turning sync on asks all five lists in one sheet; each row's "Allow" asks its own list alone:

| HealthKit type | Used for |
|---|---|
| `HKWorkoutTypeIdentifier` | The workout: its activity type, start and end, the app that recorded it (`sourceRevision.source`), its device, and its metadata (`HKTimeZone`, `HKIndoorWorkout`, `HKSwimmingLocationType`). |
| `HKQuantityTypeIdentifierActiveEnergyBurned` | The workout's active energy (`workout.getStatistic(ActiveEnergyBurned)`, else `totalEnergyBurned`, which Apple documents as "the total active energy burned during the workout"). |
| `HKQuantityTypeIdentifierDistanceWalkingRunning`, `HKQuantityTypeIdentifierDistanceCycling` | Distance, when the workout has no `totalDistance` of its own. |
| `HKQuantityTypeIdentifierStepCount` | Steps per day for Train, as HealthKit's statistics total them (see "Steps"). |
| `HKCategoryTypeIdentifierSleepAnalysis` | Sleep samples (`queryCategorySamples`, values 0 in bed, 1 asleep, 2 awake, 3 core, 4 deep, 5 REM) for the sleep log (see "Sleep"). Never added to `READ_TYPES`: HealthKit answers "already asked" for a list only when every type in it was asked, so a phone that allowed the five before sleep was read would look as if it was never asked. Turning sync on asks both lists in one sheet; the Sleep row's "Allow" asks `SLEEP_READ_TYPES` alone. |
| `HKQuantityTypeIdentifierBodyMass`, `HKQuantityTypeIdentifierBodyFatPercentage` (`WEIGHT_READ_TYPES`) | Weigh-ins with body fat (`queryQuantitySamples` in `kg` and `%`), one a day, into Weight (see "Weight and body fat"). The Weight row's "Allow" asks this list alone. Apple's percent unit is a fraction (0.25 is 25%); a value of 1 or less is multiplied by 100. |
| `HKQuantityTypeIdentifierBasalEnergyBurned` (`ENERGY_READ_TYPES`) | Resting energy per day, which with active energy per day (`queryStatisticsCollectionForQuantity`, `cumulativeSum`, `{ day: 1 }`) is the day's total burn shown on Progress. Never added to a workout or the budget. |
| `HKQuantityTypeIdentifierHeartRate` (`HEART_READ_TYPES`) | A workout's average and highest heart rate: the workout's own statistic (`getStatistic(heartRate, 'count/min')`), else `queryStatisticsForQuantity` with `discreteAverage` and `discreteMax` over the workout's own time. The fallback cannot filter by source (that needs native source objects), so a second heart-rate source recording at the same time would be counted with the watch; that is rare. |

Nothing to write (`toShare` is never passed, and the app has no `NSHealthUpdateUsageDescription`), no background delivery (the config plugin's `background: false`), no resting heart rate, routes or clinical records. HealthKit never tells an app whether a read was refused (a refused type simply returns nothing): once the question has been asked (`getRequestStatusForAuthorization` answers "unnecessary"), everything counts as readable and a refused type shows as "None in Apple Health". The screen says where to allow it: the Health app, Sharing, Apps, HealthCal. For the same reason HealthCal cannot honour a refused Active Energy read: a workout's own `totalEnergyBurned` is part of the workout sample and may come with it anyway, so the explanation sheet says so ("A workout can carry its own calorie total, which may still come with it when active energy is off; turn off workouts to keep them out") instead of promising an estimate.

Workouts from Apple's own devices carry the person's device name ("Dana's Apple Watch") and a source id with the device's UUID, `com.apple.health.<UUID>`: the iPhone's own sensors (and the Fitness app on it) as well as an Apple Watch. The kind of device comes from the sample's `sourceRevision.productType` ('Watch6,1', 'iPhone15,2'): a watch is kept as `com.apple.health.watch` ("Apple Watch"), the iPhone as `com.apple.health.iphone` ("iPhone"), and one without a product type as `com.apple.health.device` ("Apple Health"), so neither the name nor the UUID is saved in a record (`appleOrigin` in `origins.ts`).

### Steps

- **Shown, never counted as calories.** Steps appear as one line in Train's week card ("8,432 steps today, 7,950 a day this week"; for an earlier week "7,950 steps a day that week") and on the sync screen. They are never added to the budget: the activity level the budget is built on already counts everyday walking, and a walk logged or synced as a workout adds its own net calories, so adding steps would count the same walking twice. The sync screen says this once, in its explanation.
- **One count where apps overlap, and it is the store's own.** The phone and a watch (or Samsung Health and Google Fit) count the same steps. HealthCal does not merge samples itself: it asks the store for a total per day, and the store keeps one count by its own source priority, so the number on Train is the store's figure for that day (on an iPhone, the Health app's). On Android that is `aggregateGroupByPeriod({ recordType: 'Steps', timeRangeSlicer: { period: 'DAYS', length: 1 } })` (`COUNT_TOTAL` per day, `stepDaysFromPeriods` in `healthConnect.ts`); on an iPhone `queryStatisticsCollectionForQuantity('HKQuantityTypeIdentifierStepCount', ['cumulativeSum'], <first midnight>, { day: 1 })` (`stepDaysFromStatistics` in `healthKit.ts`). An earlier version spread samples over 15-minute slots and kept the largest app per slot; a review showed it counted a walk 1.75 times when one app wrote it in 15-minute pieces and another as one hour, so it was replaced.
- **The day is the phone's own calendar day**, as everywhere in the app, and both stores cut days the same way: Health Connect turns the range into local date-times in the phone's zone and names each period by its local start ('2026-11-12T00:00'); HealthKit's collection is anchored at the first day's local midnight with a one-day interval in the phone's calendar, and each interval is named by the day its middle falls on (so the days around a clock change keep their dates). A day with no steps has no entry and is left out of the average (not counted as 0). The average is over the week's earlier days, since today is not over. Under 360 px the line leaves out "this week" ("8,432 steps today, 7,950 a day"), so it stays one line; the spoken label keeps it.
- **35 days, on this phone only.** Steps are kept with the sync's memory under `healthcal-health-sync` (`steps: { days, readAt }`), the last 35 days, never in the synced store, the export, the account, the AI functions or the friends summary. Each sync re-reads the last three days (apps hand steps over late) or, after a pause, from the day before the last read; the first sync reads all 35 days. Switching sync off clears them.
- A failed or refused steps read never fails the sync: the workouts are still saved, and the days already kept stay.

### Sleep

- **What a night is.** The adapters only list raw samples (`readSleep`: `sleepFromSession` in `healthConnect.ts`, `sleepFromHealthKit` in `healthKit.ts`); every rule is pure in `sleep.ts` and tested. Only asleep time counts: light, deep, REM and asleep-without-a-stage. **Time awake and time in bed are not counted as sleep**, and an app that writes only "in bed" makes no night. Health Connect's stage numbers: 2 asleep, 4 light, 5 deep, 6 REM, and 1, 3 and 7 awake (7 is `STAGE_TYPE_AWAKE_IN_BED`, which the library's constant leaves out); a session without stages is one unstaged block. Never Health Connect's `SLEEP_DURATION_TOTAL`, which is the session's length with the time awake in it.
- **One night per wake day, from one app, never a sum.** One app's samples less than 2 hours apart are one period; overlapping samples from one app count once. The wake day is the local date of the last moment asleep (by the sample's own zone offset when the store gives one). The night is the period with the most sleep among those of 3 hours or more or touching 00:00 to 05:00; the rest are naps and are not saved. Between apps: one with stages over one without, a watch (Garmin Connect, an Apple Watch) over a phone or another app, then more sleep, then the app's id.
- **Saved in the sleep log.** A night is an ordinary `sleep` record (id from the date, as for every night) with `source: 'health'`, the store, the app it came from (never a device's or a person's name), the first and last moment asleep and the minutes per stage; no quality: only the person rates a night. A night entered by hand is never touched. A synced night is updated by a corrected reading from the same store and app that ends no earlier than the saved one, or by any reading that ranks higher; equal figures write nothing, so two phones never take turns. (Two devices on one store, an iPhone and an iPad on one Apple Health or two phones with Garmin Connect, can read the same app's night at different times: the one that has seen only the start of it ends earlier, so it never cuts the whole night short, and the later one writes the whole night once.) A night the person deleted, or whose sync they undid, is not written again (remembered on this phone under `sleep:<date>` once the save is confirmed on the device). Changing a synced night's hours makes it the person's own; rating it keeps it synced.
- **Same run, same toast.** Sleep is read in the same sync as workouts and steps; one toast says what was added ("1 workout and 2 nights of sleep added from Garmin Connect") with one Undo for both. The first sync reads 28 wake days (`SLEEP_DAYS`); later ones from the day before the last read, at least the last three; a period that ended less than 15 minutes ago is left for later. When sleep was last read (`sleepReadAt`) is kept only once the nights that read wrote are confirmed on the device, as for workouts, so a read lost to an app closed in between is read again. A failed or refused sleep read never fails the sync.
- **Why 28 days and not 35, like steps.** A deleted night's marker leaves a device 30 days after the deletion once it is uploaded (`TOMBSTONE_DAYS_SYNCED` in `src/store/persistence.ts`). A night older than that read again on a new install, a second phone or from an old export would find no record and no memory of the deletion, be added back, and its stable id would un-delete it on every device. Reading at most 28 wake days (a margin for a night's own offset being a day off the phone's calendar) means a night is never read again once its marker can be gone. Workouts have the same guard by their 30-day window, which starts at the workout while the marker's starts at the deletion. Health Connect gives only 30 days before the first grant anyway.
- **Where it shows.** The sleep log (`/sleep`: "From Garmin Connect", the stages line), Progress, the weekly recap and the morning page, which reads last night as the record dated today. The sync screen's Sleep row says "Last night 7 h 12 min, from Garmin Connect", or offers "Allow" where sleep is not shared yet.
- Sleep is never added to the calorie budget, never sent to the AI functions and never shown to friends.

### Weight and body fat

The rules are pure in `body.ts` (a to j in its header) and tested in `body.test.ts`; the run is in `engine.ts` (`engine.weight.test.ts`).

- **What a weigh-in is (a).** A weight is kept when it is 20 to 400 kg (the weigh-in sheet's own bounds), rounded to two decimals. Body fat of 1 or less is a fraction (HealthKit's percent unit) and is multiplied by 100; it is kept when it is 3 to 60%, rounded to one decimal. A reading with no app or an unreadable time is dropped.
- **Its day (b).** The local date where it was measured: Health Connect's `zoneOffset`, HealthKit's `HKTimeZone`; without one, the phone's calendar. A weigh-in at 23:30 belongs to that evening.
- **One a day, the earliest (c, e).** Per day the earliest weight across all apps; a tie at the same instant goes to the app id that sorts first. A watch app does not outrank a scale app: a weigh-in is one measurement. The earliest is stable, so a later reading never moves it and two phones converge.
- **Body fat with it (d).** The body-fat reading from the same app within 10 minutes of that weight, the nearest. A body-fat reading alone makes no weigh-in.
- **Saved in Weight (f).** An ordinary synced `weights` record (one per date, its id from the date) with `source: 'health'`, the store, the app (`origin`: a package or bundle id, never a device's or person's name), `measuredAt` (always `new Date(ms).toISOString()`, compared with `Date.parse`) and what sync wrote (`syncedKg`, `syncedBodyFatPct`). A record is a synced one only while its kg and body fat still equal those (`isSyncedWeighIn`, `src/features/trackers/weighInSource.ts`); anything else is the person's, whatever its `source` says, so an edit by an older app, setup or a restored backup is never overwritten. Per day: a date remembered as skipped (`weight:<date>`) or soft-deleted is never written; no record adds one; a record that is not synced is left alone (a value the person typed always wins); a synced one is updated only by an earlier reading that arrived late, or by the same reading corrected (its kg) or completed (its body fat); the person's waist, hip, neck and note are kept. Equal figures write nothing.
- **Deleted stays deleted, on two phones too (j).** A day the person deleted is remembered on this phone once the save is confirmed, as for sleep. Across phones the synced soft-deleted record is the memory, and when signed in weigh-ins are planned only after a data-sync round (push and pull) that ended after this run began, waited for at most 8 s (`afterPull`, `PULL_WAIT_MS`, as the morning page waits): otherwise weight is left to the next run and `weightReadAt` does not move. Workouts and sleep are not gated this way yet (see "Left out").
- **The window (g).** The first read covers 28 local days; later ones from the day before the last read, at least the last three days, never further back than 28. 28 because a deletion marker stays 30 days once uploaded (as for sleep), and it equals the learned estimate's window. `weightReadAt` is kept only once the records that read wrote are confirmed on the device.
- **One toast, one Undo (h).** Weigh-ins share the sync's one toast with workouts and nights ("1 workout, 2 nights of sleep and 3 weigh-ins added from Garmin Connect") and its one Undo, which removes the added weigh-ins, gives an updated one back as the person's own and marks every date so no phone writes it again. The first read that adds 5 or more says so: "26 weigh-ins added from Garmin Connect. They cover the last 4 weeks." What they feed is said under the sync screen's Weight row ("They feed your weight trend", and "the burn estimate your targets use" when "Learn from my data" is on): the longer toast took four lines at 360 px. A failed or refused read never fails the sync (i).
- **The profile is never written by a sync.** `profile.weightKg` and `bodyFatPct` follow a weigh-in only where they did before, by the person's own tap: saving or deleting on Weight or Body (`saveWeightRecord`, `deleteWeightRecord`, by the newest-date rule). No file under `src/features/health-sync` calls `setProfile` (`regressions.test.ts` scans for it), because the profile syncs as one object and a write before the visit's pull could replace a change made on another device. So a synced weigh-in changes the trend, the learned estimate (when on) and every screen that reads weigh-ins, not the BMR or water goal. Weight shows "Your targets use 84.0 kg. Your latest weigh-in, from Garmin Connect on Nov 18, is 82.4 kg." with **Use it for targets** (`weight-use-latest`), which applies it with Undo. Deleting a weigh-in clears the profile's body fat only when that body fat came from it, so a body fat typed in Goals stays.
- **Where it shows.** Weight's history says "From Garmin Connect" on a synced weigh-in, and its sheet says "From Garmin Connect at 06:12. Changing the weight or body fat makes it yours, and sync leaves it alone after that." Changing a note or a measurement keeps it synced. Deleting says "Weigh-in deleted. Sync will not add it again", with Undo. The sync screen's Weight row names the latest and where it came from. The morning plan's "Weigh in" row and the weigh-in reminder skip a day with a synced weigh-in by themselves.
- Weigh-ins are kept like weigh-ins entered by hand: on the device, in the person's account when they have one, and in the full export (the backup carries the new fields; the CSV's columns are unchanged). Never sent to the AI functions, never to friends.

### The day's calories burned

- **What is read.** On Android, Health Connect's day totals (`aggregateGroupByPeriod` of `TotalCaloriesBurned` and `ActiveCaloriesBurned` by day, `dayEnergyFromPeriods`): no new permission. On an iPhone, active plus resting energy per day from two statistics collections (`dayEnergyFromStatistics`); resting energy (`ENERGY_READ_TYPES`) is the one new type. The rules are pure in `dayEnergy.ts`.
- **Kept on this phone only, like steps.** The last 14 days in the sync's memory (`dayEnergy: { days, readAt }`), re-read from the day before the last read, at least the last three days. Cleared when sync is switched off. Never synced, exported, sent to the AI functions or given to friends.
- **Which days count.** A day counts only when active energy was recorded (Health Connect fills a total with a resting estimate where nothing was written, but never invents active energy), the total is above the active part (resting energy was there too), and the total is between the person's BMR and four times it.
- **Shown on Progress, never used.** With at least 5 valid days among the 7 before today (today never counts), Progress's energy card shows "Garmin Connect's estimate" (or "Health Connect's" / "Apple Health's" when several apps wrote), the average rounded to 10 kcal, and beside it HealthCal's figure for the same days: the maintenance the targets use (the learned estimate when on, else the formula) plus those days' workouts, which the watch's figure contains. It says it is shown to compare and does not change the budget, and never colours the difference or says which is right. It is never added to the budget and never informs any estimate: the watch total holds the workouts the budget already counts, and the learned estimate with real weigh-ins is the better correction. The sync screen's "Calories burned in a day" row says "About 2,640 kcal a day over 6 full days, from Garmin Connect. Shown on Progress", or "Shown on Progress after 5 full days recorded".

### Heart rate in a workout

- Read only for workouts whose details are read anyway (new ones, estimates in the recheck window, every workout on "Sync now", including the one the row's "Allow" starts), and only when heart rate is allowed. Health Connect: `aggregateRecord` of `HeartRate` over the session, from the workout's own app, kept only with at least one measurement. Apple Health: the workout's own statistic, else the statistics over its time (see the table).
- `heartFigures` (`heart.ts`): the average must be 30 to 230 bpm, the highest is kept only when it is at least the average and at most 240; both rounded to whole beats.
- Kept on the activity (`avgHeartRate`, and the new `maxHeartRate`, which a Garmin file's import now keeps too) and shown only on the workout: "Heart rate: average 142 bpm, highest 171 bpm" (`HeartRateLine`, `log-activity-heart-rate`). A stored workout without it gains it through the import's existing heart-rate update, in the one toast with Undo. Never added to calories, never in the friends summary (its test lists `maxHeartRate` among the fields it must not carry), never sent to the AI functions.

## The Health app's export file (iPhone, no HealthKit needed)

An iPhone reaches HealthCal without a paid Apple account or a Mac in two ways, the web app on the Home Screen and an `.ipa` sideloaded with a free Apple ID, and neither can count on HealthKit (a website has no access; a sideloaded copy has it only if the signing tool keeps the entitlement, which AltStore and Sideloadly do not; see "Installing on an iPhone with a free Apple ID"). Only a build signed with the entitlement can (see "State of the iPhone side"). The Health app can still hand over its data itself: **Health app > your picture > Export All Health Data** saves `export.zip`. The import screen (`/activity/import`, "Import workouts", the same flow as Garmin files and the same "Choose files" button) recognises it by its content and reads its workouts, daily steps and sleep.

### What is read, and what never is

- `export.zip` holds one XML whose root is `<HealthData>`: normally `apple_health_export/export.xml`; the folder can be localised, so the entry is found by its name `export.xml`, or else by content (the first bytes of an `.xml` entry are a HealthData export). A bare `export.xml` is read too.
- From it, only: `<Workout>` elements with their direct `<MetadataEntry>` and `<WorkoutStatistics>` children (and the statistics inside a newer file's `<WorkoutActivity>`), `<Record type="HKQuantityTypeIdentifierStepCount">` from the last 35 days (plus one day, for offsets), `<Record type="HKCategoryTypeIdentifierSleepAnalysis">` whose start is at most three days before the first of the last 28 wake days (with only the `HKTimeZone` of its own `<MetadataEntry>` children), and `<ExportDate>`. A record's type and start date are compared as bytes, so a record that is not kept is never decoded.
- Never read, never inflated: `export_cda.xml` (clinical records), `workout-routes/*.gpx` (the routes of workouts already in the XML, so never imported as workouts of their own), `electrocardiograms/*.csv`. Only their names in the ZIP's directory are seen. Inside `export.xml`, everything else (heart rate, older sleep, body measurements, the `<Me>` element with the date of birth, workout events and routes) is passed over by its first bytes: never decoded, kept or logged.
- Where a workout came from: the file names the source by the person's own device name ("Dana’s Apple Watch") and describes the device in a string that can carry identifiers. Neither is kept. Only the kind is: `com.apple.health.watch` ("Apple Watch") or `com.apple.health.iphone` ("iPhone") from the device string's `hardware`/`model` (or the name, when the device string is missing), a known app by its name (Garmin Connect, Strava, Nike Run Club, Polar Flow, ...; `exportFile.ts`), and anything else as `com.apple.health.device` ("Apple Health"), since an unknown name could be a person's. That is the same `externalId` scheme a synced workout uses, so "From Apple Watch" reads the same whichever way it arrived.
- Weigh-ins, body fat and a workout's heart rate are not read from the file yet (they come only through Health Connect or Apple Health, see "Weight and body fat"); this is left for a later round (see "Left out, and why").
- Health data goes nowhere but this phone: workouts become ordinary `activities` records and nights ordinary `sleep` records (both sync with the account like anything logged by hand), steps stay in this phone's memory.

### How a file of several gigabytes is read on a phone

`export.xml` is commonly 0.5 to 3 GB for someone with a watch, the ZIP 50 to 300 MB, sometimes zip64, and iOS writes entries with data descriptors. Nothing holds the archive or the XML in memory:

1. A picked file is a `ByteSource` (random access): `File.slice()` in a browser, an expo-file-system `FileHandle` (`file.open(FileMode.ReadOnly)`, `offset`, `readBytes`) on a phone (`pickFiles.ts`). Nothing is read when it is picked.
2. `src/features/import/zip.ts` reads the end of central directory record from the last bytes (the zip64 locator and end record when there is one), then the central directory, which gives each entry's compressed size and local header offset (0xFFFFFFFF values from the zip64 extra field). The local header's own name and extra lengths give where the data starts. The local header's sizes are never used, so data descriptors (zero sizes there) are no problem; the central directory always has the real ones.
3. Exactly that byte range is read 128 KB at a time and inflated with fflate's streaming `Inflate`; each piece of XML goes to the scanner (`src/features/import/appleExport.ts`), which carries an unfinished tag (or a multi-byte character, or an attribute) over to the next piece and keeps only what is listed above. Steps are added up as they arrive (`createStepTally`), so no step sample is kept either; sleep keeps only the stretches of the window (`createSleepTally`, a few thousand for 28 nights), never the records.
4. Between pieces the screen gets a turn (a timer), shows the share read ("42% read", once, beside the cup) and offers **Cancel**, which stops at the next piece and changes nothing. Closing the screen (X, back, another screen) during a read stops it the same way (`leaveImport` in `session.ts`): a read that was left or replaced by a new pick never writes into the session, so reopening the import shows its start, never the closed file's review.
5. The file must end with its closing `</HealthData>`. A bare `export.xml` cut short (an interrupted copy or AirDrop) still has its `<ExportDate>` at the top, so without that check its steps would replace whole days with part of them and a workout cut before its statistics would lose its energy: it is refused as "damaged or incomplete" (`HealthExportIncomplete`), and a workout left open at the cut is never handed on. A ZIP cut short has no end record: `findHealthExport` says so from its last bytes and its first entry's name (`cut-zip`), so it gets the Health export's "damaged or incomplete, export it again" message when its first entry is the export's, and "This ZIP file is cut short" otherwise, and is never read whole.

Measured with a synthetic export (`scripts/dev/make-health-export.mjs`, 1,005 MB of export.xml: heart-rate Records, 400 workouts in both generations, 41 days of steps from an iPhone and a Watch that overlap) in the built web app in Chromium at 360 x 740 with touch, from picking to the review list: 13.4 to 13.8 s and a peak JS heap of 30 to 37 MB for the ZIP (31 MB, data descriptors), 13.7 to 14.6 s and 32 to 36 MB for the same in zip64 form, 8.8 s and 32 MB for the bare 1 GB `export.xml`. The longest frame from picking to the review list was 100 to 167 ms in most runs (the review list's first draw is part of it) and 550 ms in one; before 128 KB steps were used, 1 MB steps gave 470 ms frames while reading. The synthetic XML compresses about 32 to 1, a real one about 10 to 1, so a real ZIP of the same XML is larger but the work (inflating and scanning 1 GB of text) is the same. A phone is slower than this machine; how much slower is not measured.

### Workouts

Each `<Workout>` becomes a HealthWorkout from `apple-health` (`workoutFromExport`), the shape the HealthKit adapter produces, so the rules are the same: the activity from `appleHealthTypes.ts` by the type's **name** (`HK_ACTIVITY_TYPE_NAMES`, next to the number table and tested for full coverage; an unknown name is listed as "Workout" and the review list asks), indoor and open water from `HKIndoorWorkout` and `HKSwimmingLocationType`; energy by `workoutEnergy`'s "only active" row (Apple's workout energy is active energy), so a workout costs the same from the file as from HealthKit; the record id from the start instant (`recordIdFor`), so a Garmin file, Health Connect, HealthKit and this file write one record. Two sources that recorded the same session (the watch and Garmin Connect) become one by the sync's own rule (`mergeOrigins`). They then go through the file import's pipeline (`prepareImport`, the review list, duplicates, `entriesToSave`): a workout already in HealthCal from any route is "Already imported"; a stored estimate gets the file's measured calories as an update (keeping its source); nothing is doubled, and importing the same file again adds nothing.

Both generations of the file are read: before iOS 16, `duration`, `totalDistance` and `totalEnergyBurned` with their units on the element; from iOS 16, energy and distance in `<WorkoutStatistics>` children (`ActiveEnergyBurned`, `DistanceWalkingRunning`, `DistanceCycling`, `DistanceSwimming`, ...), each with its unit (`kcal`, `Cal`, `kJ`, `J`, `cal`; `km`, `mi`, `m`, `yd`, `ft`). Dates are written as `2026-11-15 10:00:00 +0200`; the workout's day is its `HKTimeZone`'s, else the offset the date was written in.

### Steps from the file

The export has no day totals, only samples from every source, and an iPhone and a Watch both count the same walk. **"Never merge samples yourself" stays the rule for the health stores** (they give their own day totals); **a file is the one exception**, because it has no totals to ask for. The rule (`createStepTally` in `exportFile.ts`), pure and tested:

- the sources are put in one order, most steps over the window first (ties by name), the same for every day: the Health app also keeps one order of sources, the person's own, which the file does not hold;
- per source, each sample is spread over the clock hours it covers, in proportion to time;
- each hour takes the first source in that order that has steps in it, and only that one;
- a day (the phone's calendar day) is the sum of its hours.

An hour is never counted from two sources, so a walk two devices saw is counted once however they cut it into samples, and one source written in pieces of different lengths is never compared with itself. (An earlier rule took each hour's highest source; when two devices split the same walk differently across an hour's edge it took the larger part from each and gave the day more steps than either device counted.) What it cannot do: where two devices put the same steps in different hours (a sample over an hour's edge, a clock a minute off), the few steps at that edge can come from both; and in an hour where the first source has a few steps and another many, it takes the few, as the Health app does by its order. **It is an approximation of what the Health app shows**: the Health app uses the person's order and a finer grain, so a day can differ, usually by a few percent.

Saved only when the person taps **Import** (the review's footer says "Goes to 12 days, with steps for 35 days"; with no workout ticked, or none new, the button is "Save steps for 35 days"), never when the file is read: closing the review with X keeps nothing, and the Undo after Import takes the steps off again with the workouts. Kept like synced steps, on this phone only (`HealthSyncState.fileSteps`, beside `steps`: never a synced collection, never in the calorie budget, the data export, the AI functions or the friends summary), for the last 35 days. A file made at the same time as the one kept or later replaces the days it covers (`mergeFileSteps`), and the kept file's own cut-off day goes; a file made earlier than the one kept (an older export picked after a newer one) replaces nothing and only fills days the kept one has no steps for, never with its own cut-off day; an automatic sync, on a phone that has one, wins over a file for the same day. Switching sync off clears only the synced steps. Train's `StepsLine` shows them on web and iPhone too, with a second line saying where they are from and as of when ("From your Health export, Nov 16"), since a file is a snapshot; the export's own last day, cut off by the export, is left out once it is no longer today. With no steps at all (web and iPhone), Train shows one quiet link instead: "Add steps from your Health export".

### Sleep from the file

Each sleep-analysis record is one stretch of one stage from one source: `HKCategoryValueSleepAnalysisInBed`, `AsleepUnspecified` (and `Asleep` in older exports), `Awake`, `AsleepCore` (light), `AsleepDeep`, `AsleepREM`; any other value counts as nothing. `createSleepTally` (`exportFile.ts`) keeps the stretches per source (in bed and unknown are dropped at once, since they never count) and turns them into nights by **the sync's own rules** (`nightsFrom` in `sleep.ts`, the "Sleep" section above): per source the union of its stretches, its main period per wake day (naps left out), then one source per night (stages over none, a watch over a phone, more sleep), never a sum. **Time awake and time in bed are not counted as sleep**: the iPhone's "in bed" block beside the watch's night adds nothing, and a night with only "in bed" (the watch was charging) makes no night. The wake day is read in the record's `HKTimeZone` when the watch wrote one, else in the offset the date was written in; lengths come from instants, so a night across a clock change is its real length. A night that ended less than 15 minutes before the export was made is left out (it may have gone on). The 28 wake days up to today (the sync's own window, "Why 28 days" above) are read, so a night deleted longer ago than its marker lives is never added back from a file.

Where a night came from: the source's name is a grouping key in memory only (two apps of the same kind stay apart); a night keeps `store: 'file'` and the kind of device (`com.apple.health.watch`, `com.apple.health.iphone`), a known app's id (Garmin Connect), or `file:other`, never a name. The sleep log says "From your Health export" for every night from a file.

Saved only by the review's **Import** (or "Save steps and sleep" / "Save sleep for 25 nights" with no workout ticked), with the workouts' Undo. The file note says how many nights go in, how many already there stay, how many the person deleted are left out, and that time awake and in bed are not counted; the footer says "with steps for 35 days and sleep for 25 nights". What is written is `planSleep`'s plan (rule h) against every sleep record and this phone's sync memory: a day with no night gets one; a night entered by hand, one from Health Connect or Apple Health, and a deleted one are never replaced by a file; a night from an earlier file is corrected by the same source's new reading (its rating kept) or by a better one; the same file again writes nothing. Where two files read in one visit cover a day, the newer file's night is taken. Undo takes the added nights out and puts corrected ones back (`undoSleep`); a night taken out by Undo is a deleted night, so the same file leaves it out afterwards, and the undone screen says so. The file import writes nothing into the sync's per-phone memory.

### Not proven

Nobody has run this against a real export from a real iPhone here. Everything was tested against files written here in the shape the Health app is known to write (generated by `exportFixture.ts` and `make-health-export.mjs`); no real export was available to compare against. A real file could differ in:

- the folder and file names inside the ZIP (localised folder names are handled; a renamed `export.xml` is found by content only among the first three `.xml` entries);
- the ZIP itself: whether iOS writes zip64 for a large export, data descriptors (handled), a comment after the end record (handled), compression other than deflate (refused with a message);
- the DOCTYPE and its internal subset, and whether newer iOS adds elements whose names start like the kept ones;
- attribute order and quoting (the scanner relies on double quotes, as Apple writes them, and finds attributes by name);
- unit strings beyond those listed (an unknown unit leaves that figure out, so the workout falls back to an estimate);
- the activity type names of a newer iOS (they show as "Workout" and are asked about);
- how sources are named: Garmin Connect's name in the export ("Garmin Connect" or "Connect"), other apps' names, and whether the device string always carries `hardware:`;
- whether the dates' offset is the phone's zone at export time or the zone where each sample was recorded (the step tally uses the instant either way; a workout's day and a night's wake day use `HKTimeZone` first). If it is the phone's zone at export time, a record without `HKTimeZone` that was made in another zone (a night slept abroad and exported at home, most likely from a third-party app, since Apple Watch records usually carry the zone) takes its day in the export's zone, so a night that ended near midnight there can land on the next or previous wake day; the file holds nothing else to correct it from;
- sleep records: whether a real Apple Watch writes `HKTimeZone` as a child of each sleep record (the reader works without it), how Garmin Connect and other apps name themselves and their stages in the file, and whether the iPhone's "in bed" overlaps the watch's night as assumed;
- the time and memory on a real phone with a 3 GB export (measured only in desktop Chromium; the native `FileHandle` reads are written against the SDK 58 types and docs, not run on a device).

## When it runs

- When the app starts or comes to the foreground, and when the Train tab opens: at most once every 15 minutes after any attempt.
- "Sync now" on the sync screen, at any time.
- It lists the workouts of the last 30 days each time (one inexpensive call), and the steps since the last read (see "Steps"). The three energy and distance questions per workout are only asked where the answer can still change something: a new workout, or a stored estimate from the last 7 days. "Sync now" asks for all of them. (This replaces the "since the last sync minus a day" window: a short window would miss a workout that reaches Health Connect days late, and a better reading of a recent one.)
- A failure never shows a pop-up. The screen shows "The last sync did not finish", what went wrong in plain words, when it was tried and a "Try again" (or "Allow again" when the permission was taken away, or a store link when Health Connect is missing or too old). The diary is not changed by a failed sync.

## What it saves

Every workout runs through the **same pipeline as the Garmin file import** (`prepareImport`, `entriesToSave` and the duplicate rules in `src/features/import`), so a workout means the same thing whether it came from a FIT file, a CSV row or Health Connect.

- One workout is one record. The record id is the one the file import derives from the start instant (`importedEntryId(garminExternalId(start))`), so the same workout from a file and from Health Connect, on any of the person's devices, writes one record that syncs as one.
- The record is an ordinary `activities` entry with `source: 'health'`, `startedAt`, `measured` when the calories came from the device, and the app it came from in `externalId` (`garmin:<start>` for Garmin Connect, on Android or iPhone, exactly what the file import writes; `health:<package or bundle id>:<start>` for any other app, `health:com.apple.health.watch:<start>` for an Apple Watch). Train shows "From Garmin Connect · measured" or "From Apple Watch · measured" under it. So a Garmin file, Health Connect on an Android phone and Apple Health on an iPhone write one record for one workout, and it reaches the person's other devices through the normal sync.
- The day is the one where the workout was done: the record's own zone offset decides it, as the file import's offset does. A workout that crosses midnight belongs to the day it started.
- Exercise types map to the app's activities in `exerciseTypes.ts` (Health Connect) and `appleHealthTypes.ts` (HealthKit's `HKWorkoutActivityType`, with indoor cycling, walking and rowing and open-water swimming read from the workout's metadata), in the same spirit as `sports.ts` for Garmin's sports. A type that could be several activities ("Other workout", "Skating", "Paddling") or that the table does not know waits for the person to choose; the choice is remembered for later workouts of the same kind.
- New workouts are saved by themselves, with one toast ("2 workouts added from Garmin Connect") whose Undo removes exactly those.

### Calories (exercise adds net calories, never gross)

One workout gets one net figure whichever way it arrives (Health Connect, a FIT file, a CSV row), and the figure does not change when the other source arrives later.

| Health Connect has | Gross (the session's total) | Net (added to the day) |
|---|---|---|
| total (with or without active) | total | total minus the resting share: exactly the file import's rule for Garmin's total |
| only active | active plus the person's resting rate (BMR) for those minutes, which is what the device's own total would be | gross minus the resting share |
| neither | the app's own MET estimate, shown as an estimate with its range | the estimate's net |

Apple Health has no workout total with resting included (HealthCal reads basal energy only as a day's total for Progress, never inside a workout), so an Apple Health workout with active energy always takes the "only active" row, and one without takes the estimate. A workout that also arrives as a Garmin file keeps whichever figure was saved first, as for Health Connect: the record id is the same, and a stored measured reading is not replaced.

The resting share is the person's daily baseline (`formulaTdee`) / 1440 x the session's minutes, the same figure a hand-logged session uses. A total that is not above the active figure is not used.

Why the active figure is not added as it is: Health Connect's "active" energy is energy above the device's BMR, while the day's budget already holds the baseline (BMR times the everyday activity factor) for every minute, the workout's included. Adding "active" would count the gap between the two twice: about 30 kcal an hour for a typical person, about 350 kcal on a 9-hour hike. This differs from the first brief ("with both: net = active") and was changed after a review found the same Garmin run got 629 kcal from Health Connect and 600 from its FIT file.

### Duplicates

- **Two apps, one workout.** Sessions from different apps that cover the same time (half or more of the shorter one) are one workout. The one with measured calories is kept; when both or neither have them, Garmin Connect. If the phone's reading was already saved and the watch's reading arrives later, the watch's reading takes its place (in the same toast, with Undo), whether the two starts are seconds or minutes apart: it is saved under its own id (the id a Garmin file gives that run) and says "From Garmin Connect". If the person had changed the phone's reading (its length, effort or activity), the watch's reading waits in "Needs a look" instead, with Replace and Skip.
- **Logged by hand.** A workout that might be a session the person logged by hand (same day, same kind of activity, length within 25%, logged after the workout started) is **not** saved by itself. It waits in "Needs a look" on the sync screen with the file import's choices: Replace (the hand log is soft-deleted and its note moves to the workout), Keep both, or Skip. Train shows a small count ("Sync, 2 to look at").
- **Already stored.** Imported before from a file, or synced on another phone: nothing is added.
- **Deleted.** A workout the person deleted is never brought back: the soft-deleted record stays as a marker, and this phone also remembers each synced workout, so it stays deleted even after the marker is gone (after 24 hours without an account, 30 days with one). A skipped workout is not asked again.
- **The order of the two saves.** The diary (the app's store, written to the device a moment after a change) and this phone's memory (its own key) are saved apart, so a workout is remembered as saved only once the diary holding it is confirmed on the device (`rememberSaved` in `engine.ts`: `flushPersistence()` and no failed save, within 10 seconds). A memory that ran ahead of the diary would turn a workout lost to an app closed in between into one "the person deleted", never added again; when the save cannot be confirmed nothing is remembered and the next sync tries again, with nothing new to see. The other half-state heals: every sync, also one skipped as too soon, remembers the synced workouts the diary holds, live or deleted, that this phone does not remember yet (`unremembered` in `state.ts`: any `source: 'health'` record of the last 45 days not waiting in "Needs a look", so also those from the Health export and those another phone added through the account). Undo remembers what it took out at once, so an app closed right after it does not bring the workouts back. While a confirmation waits the sync is `saving`, which the screen does not show and the updater waits for as it does for a run.
- **A better reading later.** If a stored workout is an estimate and Health Connect now has its calories (or distance), the stored record is updated in place, keeping its id, day, activity and the person's notes, as the file import's update rule does. A record the person changed (its length, or its effort or activity, which turns measured calories into an estimate) is left alone, by the file import's own rule (`personEdited`). Undo puts the old numbers back and that change is not made again.
- **Accidental starts.** A session under a minute is left out (counted as unusable on the screen); one longer than a day is a recording that was never stopped and is left out too.

## What is stored, and where

- **The workouts** are ordinary `activities` records. Without an account they stay on the device. With an account they sync to the person's own private space on the HealthCal server, like every other session they log. They are part of the full export.
- **The nights of sleep** are ordinary `sleep` records, kept and synced like nights entered by hand, and part of the full export.
- **The weigh-ins** are ordinary `weights` records, kept and synced like weigh-ins entered by hand, and part of the full export. **Heart rate** is kept on the workout's record.
- **The day's calories burned** (the last 14 days), when weight was last read (`weightReadAt`) and which days' weigh-ins were deleted or undone (`weight:<date>`) are kept in the per-phone memory below.
- **The switch and its memory** (on or off, last synced, the last error, the apps found, the "Needs a look" list, which workouts were saved or skipped and which days of sleep were deleted or undone, when sleep was last read, activity choices, and the last 35 days of steps) are kept **only on this phone**, in on-device storage under the key `healthcal-health-sync`, never in the synced settings: the permission belongs to one phone. It is cleared when the person signs out or erases the data on the device.
- **Never sent anywhere else.** Health data, steps and sleep included, is never sent to the AI food analysis function (it only receives the photo or description the person submits). Friends see only what the friends summary already allows for any workout: minutes, sessions, streak, and recent workouts as the catalogue name and emoji, length, distance and date. Never calories, weight, heart rate, notes, the time of day, the device or the app it came from. No analytics, no ads, no third parties.

## Turning it off

Switch it off on the sync screen: nothing more is read, and the "Needs a look" list, the kept steps and the kept day's calories are cleared. Workouts, nights and weigh-ins already added stay, in Train, the sleep log and Weight, where any of them can be deleted. To take the permission away as well, open Health Connect > App permissions > HealthCal, or on an iPhone the Health app > Sharing > Apps > HealthCal (the screen's "Open Health Connect" / "Open Apple Health" row opens it). If the permission is taken away while sync is on, the next sync says so on the screen and offers "Allow again".

## How the iPhone side is bound

Everything below `adapter.ios.ts` is plain TypeScript tested against fixtures in the library's own shapes; `adapter.ios.ts` hands it the real library. What is in place:

1. The packages, installed with `npx expo install`: `@kingstinct/react-native-healthkit` 16.2.0 (with `@react-native-healthkit/core` 16.2.0 as its dependency) and `react-native-nitro-modules` 0.37.2 for its peer (`>=0.35`).
2. Its config plugin in `app.json` `plugins`. It adds the `com.apple.developer.healthkit` entitlement and the read text; `false` leaves out the write text and the background-delivery entitlement:
   ```json
   ["@kingstinct/react-native-healthkit", {
     "NSHealthShareUsageDescription": "HealthCal reads the workouts, steps and sleep you choose from Apple Health to fill your training week and sleep log. It never writes to Apple Health and never sends this data to the AI functions.",
     "NSHealthUpdateUsageDescription": false,
     "background": false
   }]
   ```
3. `src/features/health-sync/adapter.ios.ts`, the binding: it imports `isHealthDataAvailable`, `getRequestStatusForAuthorization`, `requestAuthorization`, `queryWorkoutSamples` and `queryStatisticsCollectionForQuantity` from the library and passes them to `createHealthKitAdapter` (`healthKitAdapter.ts`), with `openHealth` opening the Health app (`x-apple-health://`, else the app's own settings). It is the only file that imports the module; Metro picks it by its `.ios` extension, so Android and the browser never load it.
4. `APPLE_HEALTH_IN_APP` is `true` in `src/features/health-sync/reach.ts`, so the Profile row, the sync screen and About name Apple Health; `reach.test.ts` fails when the flag and `adapter.ios.ts` disagree.

After an upgrade of the library (or of Expo), check in this order:

- `npx tsc --noEmit`: `adapter.ios.ts` passes the real functions to `createHealthKitAdapter`, so a changed call fails here. The shapes in `healthKit.ts` are written out by hand, so compare them with the declarations listed below as well.
- `npx vitest run src/features/health-sync` and `npx expo-doctor`.
- `npx expo prebuild --platform ios` in a scratch copy of the repository (never in this one: `ios/` is generated): the entitlements file has `com.apple.developer.healthkit` and no `com.apple.developer.healthkit.background-delivery`; `Info.plist` has `NSHealthShareUsageDescription` with the text above and no `NSHealthUpdateUsageDescription`.

## Installing on an iPhone with a free Apple ID (which signing tools keep Apple Health)

What was checked, 16 November 2026, by reading each tool's source or changelog. Nothing in this section has been tried on a phone yet.

- **Apple allows HealthKit for a free account.** Apple's table of capabilities by membership ("Supported capabilities (iOS)", developer.apple.com/help/account/reference/supported-capabilities-ios) marks HealthKit as available to all three: the Apple Developer Program, the Enterprise Program and "Apple Developer" (a free Apple Account that agreed to the developer agreement). So a free account can have it, through a provisioning profile whose App ID has HealthKit enabled, as Xcode does when the capability is added with a free personal team. No statement from Apple was found that iOS refuses HealthKit from a free profile.
- **A tool has to ask for it, and it can only know to ask from the app.** A signing tool registers an App ID for the person's account, switches capabilities on for it, fetches a provisioning profile and signs. The tools that handle HealthKit decide what to switch on from the entitlements inside the app's own signature. An app built with `CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO` has no signature and so asks for nothing. `.eas/build/ios-unsigned.yml` therefore ends by giving the app an ad-hoc signature (no identity, no account) with one entitlement, `com.apple.developer.healthkit`, and the build fails if the packaged app does not carry it. The push entitlement that prebuild writes (`aps-environment`) is left out: a free account cannot have it and the app's reminders are local.
- **Impactor (github.com/khcrysalis/Impactor; Windows with iTunes, macOS, Linux): may work.** It matches the app's entitlement keys against Apple's list of capabilities and switches on each one that is not on its list of capabilities a free account cannot have (`crates/plume_core/src/developer/v1/capabilities.rs`; HealthKit is not on that list), then signs with the entitlements of the profile Apple returns (`crates/plume_utils/src/signer.rs`). So the signed app has HealthKit exactly if Apple's free profile carries it once the App ID has the capability.
- **xtool (github.com/xtool-org/xtool; Linux, Windows through WSL, macOS): may work.** Its capability table marks HealthKit as allowed for free teams (`DeveloperServicesCapability(.healthkit, isFree: true)`), it switches the capability on and signs with the app's own entitlements (`Sources/XKit/DeveloperServices/App IDs/DeveloperServicesAddAppOperation.swift`); `xtool install <ipa>` installs a prebuilt `.ipa`. If Apple's free profile does not carry HealthKit, iOS refuses the install instead of dropping the entitlement. Setup is a developer's job (Swift, usbmuxd, USB passthrough on Windows).
- **AltStore, SideStore and Sideloader do not give it.** AltStore signs with the entitlements of the profile it fetches, and before that switches on App ID features only for App Groups and Inter-App Audio (AltSign, `AltSign/Capabilities/ALTCapabilities.m` and `AltSign/Signing/ALTSigner.mm`; `updateFeatures` in AltStore's `FetchProvisioningProfilesOperation.swift`). SideStore's AltSign fork has the same two mappings. Dadoum's Sideloader switches on App Groups only and signs with the profile's entitlements (`source/sideload/sign.d`). So with them the profile, and the signed app, have no `com.apple.developer.healthkit`, whatever the `.ipa` carries.
- **Sideloadly says custom entitlements are for paid accounts.** Its changelog (sideloadly.io/changelog), v0.60.0: "Added support for custom app entitlements (Apple Developer Program only); this is a Patreon-exclusive feature." It is closed source. v0.71.0 says "Support for free Apple Accounts is coming in the next version", so this may change.
- **What the app does when the permission is missing.** HealthKit refuses such a copy with "Missing com.apple.developer.healthkit entitlement." The adapter turns that into `not-entitled`, and the sync screen says, before the switch is touched: "Apple Health is not available in this install", with why and an "Import your Health export" button (see "The Health app's export file"). Garmin files still work there. So a person finds out which kind of copy they have by opening Workout sync: the switch and Apple's own Health access sheet, or that message.
- **What is certain to work.** A build signed through an Apple Developer Program account (99 USD a year: TestFlight, the App Store, or EAS internal distribution with a registered device; a friend's membership through TestFlight is the same thing), or Xcode on a Mac with the person's own free account and the HealthKit capability added (7-day signing, as for any free install).
- **Unknown until tried on a phone.** Whether Apple's free development profile really carries `com.apple.developer.healthkit` once the App ID has HealthKit (everything above about Impactor and xtool depends on it); whether Apple's capability list names HealthKit by the key Impactor matches on; whether a later Sideloadly honours HealthKit for free accounts; and whether HealthKit already refuses `getRequestStatusForAuthorization` without the entitlement (the screen then shows the message at once) or only `requestAuthorization` (it shows it when the switch is turned on). Both paths are handled and tested.
- **Trying it with Impactor on Windows.** Install Apple's iTunes and Impactor (its GitHub releases page). Connect the iPhone by cable, unlock it and tap Trust. In Impactor sign in with the Apple ID and give it the `.ipa`. On the iPhone: Settings, Privacy & Security, Developer Mode on (the phone restarts); then Settings, General, VPN & Device Management, trust the Apple ID. Open HealthCal, Profile, Workout sync. A switch that leads to Apple's Health access sheet means it works; "Apple Health is not available in this install" means the profile did not carry it. The install lasts 7 days, like every free one.

## Not yet proven: weight, the day's calories and heart rate

Nothing of this round has run on a phone. Built and tested against fixtures in the libraries' own shapes (`react-native-health-connect` 4.1.3, `@kingstinct/react-native-healthkit` 16.2.0) and against the in-memory store in a browser. To see on the owner's phone:

- That Garmin Connect writes `Weight` and `BodyFat` to Health Connect, and `BodyMass` and `BodyFatPercentage` to Apple Health (the source is the press quoting Garmin's FAQ, and a 2021 report for iPhone). Owner: read Garmin Connect's Health Connect list of shared data.
- That Garmin writes `HeartRate` samples inside its exercise sessions with its own origin, and what Apple Health gets for a Garmin workout (a workout statistic, or only samples in its window).
- That Garmin's daily calories arrive as `TotalCaloriesBurned` and `ActiveCaloriesBurned` records that the day aggregates total, and what `dataOrigins` lists when Health Connect fills resting time. Whether Garmin writes resting energy to Apple Health; without it the iPhone shows no watch figure.
- That `getRequestStatusForAuthorization` answers "should request" for each new list on a phone that allowed the old ones, and "unnecessary" after the combined first sheet.
- That Apple's percent unit is a fraction (the rule copes either way).
- That a Health Connect request listing permissions the installed manifest lacks only leaves them ungranted. Until `app.json` has them, no build or update with this code may reach the runtime "2" apps.
- Whether a workout's calories hold part of the day's energy. A workout's energy is Health Connect's aggregate over the session window from the workout's app; if Garmin writes its day energy as day-long or hour-long records, Health Connect prorates a slice of them into that window. On a day with a Garmin workout: open Health Connect > Data > Total calories burned > Garmin Connect, note each entry's start and end, and compare the workout's calories in HealthCal with Garmin Connect's. If the entries span hours or the whole day, change `details()` in `adapter.android.ts` to read the records in the session window from that app and sum only those lying entirely inside it.
- That "the earliest weigh-in of the day" matches the owner's habit when they weigh more than once a day.
- Play's approval of the re-filed declaration (up to a week).

## Left out, and why

- **Resting heart rate.** Sources disagree on whether Garmin writes it to Health Connect, it has no calorie role, and Play asks for the fewest types.
- **Heart-rate variability, SpO2, breathing rate, skin and body temperature, VO2 max:** Garmin does not write them.
- **Garmin's own scores** (Body Battery, stress, training load and readiness, intensity minutes, sleep score): they never leave Garmin Connect and neither store has a type for them.
- **Floors and elevation:** already inside the activity level.
- **Hydration and nutrition:** logged in HealthCal; reading them back would count them twice.
- **Lean, bone and water mass, BMI, blood pressure:** no feature uses them.
- **Weigh-ins and workout heart rate from the Health app's export file:** not small (the byte scanner would keep two more record types, the review needs counts, and a file-versus-store rule needs its own tests). Left for a later round.
- **The watch's day calories informing the budget or the learned estimate:** never (see "The day's calories burned").
- **Gating workouts and sleep on the data-sync pull as weigh-ins are:** they share the exposure (a phone that has not pulled a deletion yet could write the record back), and this is a separate change recommended for the next round with the same `afterPull`.
