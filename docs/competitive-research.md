# What other health apps do: a survey of 33 apps

Research for HealthCal's design, carried out on 13 and 14 November 2026.

## What we looked at

Thirty-three apps were studied, one researcher each: fifteen calorie and nutrition trackers, nine training and activity apps, four recovery and all-in-one apps, and four hydration and fasting apps. One further app was found by searching for what is trending rather than by name (see the last row of the table: the "CalFit" the project owner mentioned is a real App Store app and was included).

For each app the researcher read the current App Store listing, the official site and recent reviews, and **opened and looked at the real store screenshots**: 235 images in total, between 3 and 8 per app. Every note separates what was seen in a screenshot from what was only read or assumed.

Two cautions when reading this:

- Store screenshots are chosen by the app's marketing team. They show the best screens, not the awkward ones, and say little about how many taps a flow takes.
- Onboarding flows, pricing detail and user complaints come from text sources (listings, review articles, forum summaries), several of them written by competitors. Treat those as directional.

## Patterns the strongest apps share

**The day at a glance**

- *A week strip of small day rings above the main number* (MyFitnessPal, FatSecret, SnapCalorie, FoodNoms, CalFit, Lose It!). It shows the week's shape and doubles as the way to move between days.
- *The main pillars visible without scrolling* (Healthify, Fitbit, Fastic, Apple Fitness, WHOOP, Bevel): calories first, then water, activity and sleep as a compact row of rings or bars, not a long stack of cards.
- *The add button leads with the likely next log* (MacroFactor, Lifesum, Strava, Hevy): "usual for dinner" before a menu of methods.
- *One suggestion that can be acted on in place* (Healthify, Lifesum, WHOOP, Bevel): a coach line with a button that does the thing, not a link to another screen.

**Logging**

- *Photo logging ends in a list you can correct* (MyFitnessPal, Cal AI, SnapCalorie, FoodNoms, Cronometer): each detected food with its portion, swap suggestions, and one commit button. Apps that show only a total are the ones users distrust.
- *Barcode scanning confirms on a card over the live camera and keeps scanning* (MyNetDiary, Lifesum, Carb Manager, Foodvisor), and checks the user's own foods before the public database.
- *After a one-tap add, the row turns into an amount stepper* (FatSecret, MacroFactor, Cal AI, Carb Manager), so the common correction ("one and a half, not one") needs no second screen.
- *Strength apps are built around repeating a routine* (Hevy, Strong, Fitbod): start last week's workout in one tap, see last time's numbers in each row, a rest timer that starts when a set is ticked, and a finish summary with personal records.

**Staying with it**

- *A weekly check-in as a ritual* (MacroFactor, Lifesum, Carb Manager, Strava, Lose It!): one screen on Monday with the week's numbers, a win and a focus. Strava was criticised for putting its recap behind a paywall.
- *Streaks that forgive* (Gentler Streak, Bevel, Oura): rest days count, a missed day does not reset everything, and illness or travel can be marked.
- *Baselines beside targets* (Gentler Streak, WHOOP, Bevel, Strava): "your usual" next to the goal, so a day is read against the person's own pattern.
- *Reminders that can be answered from the notification* (WaterMinder, Waterllama, Strong, FoodNoms): "+250 ml" on the reminder itself.

**How the best-looking ones look**

- *One colour per metric, used as atmosphere* (Oura, Gentler Streak, WHOOP, Bevel, Lifesum): a soft wash of the metric's colour behind the top of each screen instead of a heavy coloured header.
- *A condensed display face for the numbers* (WHOOP, Zero, Garmin, Apple Fitness, Fitbod): figures large and tight, labels small.
- *Charts that show the target as a band* (FoodNoms, Bevel, MacroFactor), not a single dashed line, and let you touch a bar to read it.
- *The user's own meal photos as the imagery* (Cal AI, SnapCalorie, Lifesum) in place of stock illustration.
- *Custom glyphs and a mascot or mark* (Waterllama, Gentler Streak, Hevy) where others use system emoji.

**What users resent, across the category**

Paywalls that take away something that used to be free (barcode scanning in MyFitnessPal and Lose It!), advertising in the logging flow, subscriptions that are hard to cancel (Noom, Cal AI), crowd-sourced food entries that contradict each other, and photo estimates presented as exact.

## What HealthCal took from this

Built in the round that followed the survey (see the commit history for each):

- A week strip of day rings on Today, the four pillars above the fold, and meal rows that read at a glance.
- "Usual for this meal" at the top of the add sheet, and a coach line with actions that do the thing.
- Amount steppers on search rows after a one-tap add; saved meals that can be built, edited and logged by the serving.
- Barcode scanning that checks your own foods first and confirms on a card over the camera.
- A photo review step with swap suggestions and "what did it get wrong?" re-estimation.
- Strength routines (repeat a workout in one tap), a rest timer and a finish summary with personal records.
- A weekly check-in screen, an overview grid on Progress, target bands on charts, a consistency calendar.
- An onboarding step for the user's day and reminders, and reminders that can be answered from the notification.
- A soft per-metric colour behind each screen, a condensed face for headline numbers, and the Blossom theme.

Looked at and left for later, with the reason:

- **Voice logging** (MyFitnessPal, Lose It!, Bevel): needs a speech-recognition module that could not be verified in this environment.
- **Scanning a nutrition label to create a food** (SnapCalorie, FoodNoms, MacroFactor): a good fit for the existing analysis function; not started.
- **Home-screen widgets and a watch app** (WaterMinder, FoodNoms, Apple Fitness): native targets outside the shared codebase.
- **Sync with Apple Health and Health Connect** (most of the category): needs native builds and device testing; the place where other apps double count, so it deserves care.
- **Pausing goals for illness or travel**, **a weekly focus**, **share cards** and **badge art**: worthwhile, lower priority than getting the numbers right.
- **Micronutrients beyond the eight tracked** (Cronometer's strength): depends on a richer food database than the bundled one.
