# Bundled food data

The bundled reference list (`src/data/foods.ts`) holds 467 foods, available offline. Each food is `verified: true` exactly when it names the one record its values come from, and the record decides the line behind the food's "Reference" badge.

| Source | Field | Foods |
|---|---|---|
| USDA FoodData Central: SR Legacy 2018-04 and FNDDS survey foods 2024-10-31 | `fdcId` | 329 |
| Israel's national nutrition database (Tzameret), Ministry of Health, published on data.gov.il; foods updated October 2022 | `ilCode` (the record's `Code`) | 118 (55 of them recipes the Ministry computed, listed in `NATIONAL_RECIPES`; 63 Israeli products' labels, `makor` 3, all listed in `NATIONAL_LABELS`) |
| Neither: estimates from a typical label or recipe | none | 20 |

Values are per 100 g; drinks too (the water screen converts ml with the first serving that names a volume).

Food detail says where the values come from (`referenceExplain` in `src/features/food/helpers.ts`). A national food names "the Israeli Ministry of Health" in every case: a recipe "computed by the Israeli Ministry of Health", a product "from the product's label as recorded there". A food with neither a USDA nor a national record reads "An estimate from a typical label or recipe, not a measured record."

## Carbohydrate

USDA stores total carbohydrate (by difference, fibre included). The national database stores available carbohydrate, without fibre. So a national food's `carbs` is the record's `carbohydrates` plus its `total_dietary_fiber`, and its `fiber` is that fibre. Where a record has no fibre value, it is used only when its energy equals 4P + 4C + 9F within 3% (no fibre was counted); its carbohydrate is then stored as given and `fiber` is left out. Energy is checked by each source's own convention: 4/4/9 (+7 for alcohol) for USDA, and 4P + 4(C - fibre) + 2 fibre + 9F for national foods.

kcal is whole, grams are to 0.1, sodium is whole mg, sugar is capped at carbohydrate. A value the record leaves empty is left out, never guessed. A value the record contradicts is not stored either: limonana (8835), whose sugar 0 sits beside 10.2 g of carbohydrate that can only be sugar, and eshel (84) and sour cream 15% (285), whose sugar 0.1 sits beside 4.2 g and 3.3 g of milk carbohydrate that can only be lactose.

## Which record

- A national record is added only when the app has no row for the same food. A verified USDA row for the same food is kept (it gets search names in `src/data/searchNames.ts`; halva is one). A national row may sit beside a USDA row only when it is a different product bought by a different label (cottage cheese 5%, milk 1%, the bakery pitas, the baked white-cheese cake). The one USDA row replaced by a national one is pickled herring: Israeli label records exist (1227), and the USDA value was 30% above them.
- Compare the record with its siblings: the other national records of the same dish or product (found by the Hebrew name's head words), kcal per 100 g. A record more than 25% from their median is replaced by a mainstream sibling, or kept with a stated reason (a different fat level, a different product). This is how the first hamin record (684, twice every other cholent) was caught.
- Only `makor` 3 (an Israeli product's label) or 4 (a recipe the Ministry computed). Never `makor` 1 or 6 (copies of USDA records: add the USDA record with its `fdcId` instead), never `makor` 2 (foreign products), never an `FFQ-` row. A new row needs a household unit and a place in its category's section.
- Where the database has one recipe in several oils, one row is used. A record whose Hebrew and English names disagree is never given a flavour either name contradicts (rugelach 2445 is plain "Rugelach").
- English names follow the list's style: noun first, qualifiers after a comma or in brackets, COOKED/DRY where it matters. A dish keeps its own name with a plain gloss ("Malawach, fried"). A packaged product is named by what it is, with the product's name in brackets ("Peanut puffs, corn-based (Bamba)"). A new name must not take the first result of an English search from an existing food: if it would, move the generic word out of the head of the name ("Tuna with mayonnaise (tuna salad)"). The photo review's suggestions (`matchFoods`) read the first word of a name too: "Yogurt with fruit, 3% fat" and "Cheesecake, baked, made with 5% quark" are named so that "Fruit salad" and "Cheese omelette" do not offer them first (`ai-items.tsv`). A place may be part of a dish's usual name (Turkish coffee, Druze pita, Jerusalem grill); the rule is that no name is given a place to mark where a food comes from.
- The 348 foods the list held before the Israeli foods were added keep their English names ("Milk, whole", "Cheddar cheese", "Greek yogurt, plain, nonfat"): adding foods must not change what an English search shows for an everyday food. The one exception is labneh, "full fat" before and "9% fat" now, its record's level. A new row whose product comes in several fat percentages (milk, yogurts, creams, cheeses, minced meat) states its own percentage in its English name, from the record's fat per 100 g ("Bulgarian cheese, 24% fat", "Cottage cheese, 9% fat", "Milk, 1% fat"). The food search reads a number followed by אחוז or אחוזים as that number with a percent sign, in every pass ("חלב 3 אחוז" gives exactly what "חלב 3%" gives: whole milk, through its own search name; `percentSigns` in `src/lib/hebrew.ts`). The parts pass never searches a percentage alone.

## Servings (labels)

- Every serving of a national food is a unit of that food in the national unit table (`yehidot_mida_lemitzrachim`: food `mmitzrach`, unit `mida`, grams `mishkal`), at most four, never "100 g" (apart from a weight-only row, below). Nothing is derived or kept from elsewhere; a food with no plausible household unit is not added.
- A plain unit (100, יחידה) heavier than 120 g states its grams: merguez's piece is "1 serving (133 g)" and lachuch's "1 piece (175 g)", so a large piece is never read as an ordinary one.
- Weight-only servings: a row whose record has no household unit a person would mean may state its servings as weights, "<n> g" at exactly n grams, when the record has the gram unit (700). Only the rows in `WEIGHT_ONLY` in `scripts/ops/check-national-foods.mjs`, each with its reason, may do this; today one: in-shell sunflower seeds (1877), whose units are a 2.7 g tbsp and a 1 g tsp, served as "50 g" and "100 g" (the snack is eaten by the bag or the handful). Such a row may repeat the implicit 100 g; every other row may not.
- Labels come from the unit's own name. A size word (small, large, mini, thin, thick) is allowed only when the unit's name has it, or when the label states its grams in brackets: baklava's 76 g medium unit is "1 piece (76 g)" beside its 41 g "1 small piece", merguez's only piece (133 g) is "1 serving (133 g)", Druze pita's only unit (named large) is "1 large pita (172 g)". The first serving is the one a person most often means. No serving is invented: cheese bourekas keeps its record's 50 g piece, because no national cheese-bourekas record has a bakery-size unit.
- English labels by unit: 100 "1 piece" or the food's noun (pita, roll, bar...), 101 "1 mini ...", 102 "1 small ...", 103 "1 ..." (medium is the default), 104 "1 large ...", 200 "1 cup" (a drink: "1 cup (240 ml)"), 207 "1 cup grated", 300 "1 tbsp" up to 25 g else "1 serving spoon", 301 "1 level tbsp", 302 "1 heaped tbsp" up to 25 g else "1 heaped serving spoon", 400 "1 tsp", 500/502 "1 slice", 501 "1 thin slice", 503 "1 thick slice", 600 "1 tub" ("1 pot" for a dessert), 601 "1 mini tub", 602 "1 small tub", 800 "1 serving", 801 "1 small serving" ("1 small bowl" for a soup), 802 "1 serving" ("1 bowl" for a soup), 803 "1 large serving", 903 "1 bag" ("1 small bag" when the record also has a large package, 912: Bamba's 25 g bag), 904 "1 carton", 905 "1 single pack", 906 "1 box" (or "1 tub"), 911 "1 small bag", 912 "1 large bag", 1006 "1 small bowl", 1208 "1 finger". A new unit gets its label in `labelFits` in `scripts/ops/check-national-foods.mjs`.
- "tbsp" means a measuring spoon: 4 to 25 g; "tsp" 1 to 10 g. A national spoon heavier than 25 g is a "serving spoon".
- A soup's portions (units 801 and 802, small and medium portions) are labelled "1 small bowl" and "1 bowl" on purpose: that is how a soup is served. The check script allows it for a food whose name says soup.
- A drink names its volume in millilitres in its first volume serving ("1 serving (140 ml)", "1 cup (240 ml)", "1 small cup (75 ml)" for Turkish coffee), and that serving weighs 0.90 to 1.15 g per ml.

## Adding a food

From USDA: find the record in the FNDDS survey release (or SR Legacy), add an `f(...)` row in its category's section with the record's values by the rules above and its `fdcId`, servings from the record's portions where one matches. A value the record does not report is left out. List the new row in `FNDDS_ADDED` or `SR_ADDED` in `scripts/ops/check-national-foods.mjs`, so the check proves it against the release (`--fndds`, `--sr`).

From the national database: get the tables (below), find the food's `Code`, check its `makor`, compute the values by the carbohydrate rule, pick at most four units from the unit table with the labels above, and add an `il(...)` row in its category's section with the code. Add the code to `NATIONAL_RECIPES` when `makor` is 4, and to `NATIONAL_LABELS` when `makor` is 3 (every product record is there, so its source line says "from the product's label as recorded there"). Give the food its search names in `src/data/searchNames.ts`, on its own line in the order of `foods.ts`; at least one must share a word with the record's Hebrew name. Update the counts in the header of `foods.ts`, `scripts/check-foods.mjs` and `src/data/data-integrity.test.ts`. Then run every check below.

## Checks

```bash
node --experimental-strip-types scripts/check-foods.mjs     # consistency only: ids, records named once, energy, spoons, drinks, weight labels, counts
npx vitest run src/data                                      # the same as tests, plus the search names
node --experimental-strip-types scripts/ops/check-national-foods.mjs \
  --foods=<dir>/mitzrachim.json --units=<dir>/yehidot_mida_lemitzrachim.json \
  [--fndds=<dir>/FoodData_Central_survey_food_csv_2024-10-31] [--sr=<dir>/FoodData_Central_sr_legacy_food_csv_2018-04] \
  [--foods-ts=<copy of foods.ts>] [--names-ts=<copy of searchNames.ts>]
```

`check-foods.mjs` checks consistency only: it never compares a value with its source record, so a wrong value that still agrees with its own macros passes it. `check-national-foods.mjs` is the check against the sources. It is run by hand, not in CI, because the source tables are not in the repository. For every food with an `ilCode` it finds the record, refuses a `makor` other than 3 or 4 and an `FFQ-` row, recomputes every value (within 0.05 g, 0.5 kcal, 0.5 mg of the record), checks that every serving is a unit of that food at those grams with its label, checks the spoon and drink rules, checks that at least one of the food's Hebrew search names shares a word (after the app's `fold`) with the record's Hebrew name unless the food is in its short `NAME_EXCEPTIONS` list with a reason (it prints every pair), and checks that `NATIONAL_RECIPES` is exactly the `makor` 4 codes and `NATIONAL_LABELS` exactly the `makor` 3 codes. With `--fndds` it checks the six foods added from FNDDS in November 2026 against `food_nutrient.csv`, and each of their servings against the record's own portions (`food_portion.csv`) or the units of the national record of the same food that the header of `foods.ts` names. With `--sr` it checks the foods added from SR Legacy in the second fix round (`SR_ADDED`: pumpkin seeds in the shell, 168601, the record national record 8250 copies) the same way, and also that a value the record does not report (its sugar) is left out. It also checks the weight-only rule (`WEIGHT_ONLY`) and that a plain unit above 120 g states its grams. It reads CSV or JSON. `--foods-ts` and `--names-ts` (and `--foods-ts` on `check-foods.mjs`) run the checks against a copy, so a planted fault can be shown to be caught. The date it last passed is in the header of `foods.ts`.

The national tables are on data.gov.il's CKAN API (`datastore_search`), resource ids: foods `c3cb0630-0650-46c1-a068-82d575c094b2`, units per food `755d28c0-75f7-40e1-9c8c-ecdd106f9b2d`, unit names `98fb46fe-e8de-4067-94d2-b0a8ea4269da`. Page through `https://data.gov.il/api/3/action/datastore_search?resource_id=<id>&limit=10000&offset=<n>` with a generic client name and a pause between requests, and save the result outside the repository. FNDDS: `https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/fdc-datasets/FoodData_Central_survey_food_csv_2024-10-31.zip`. SR Legacy: `https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/fdc-datasets/FoodData_Central_sr_legacy_food_csv_2018-04.zip`.
